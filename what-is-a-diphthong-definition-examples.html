What Is a Diphthong?

What is a diphthong? Diphthongs are specific sounds that are found in some English words in which two vowel sounds bleed into each other. While diphthongs are very common, many people do not notice them when they are speaking. Diphthongs can be a particular challenge for those who are learning English as a second language. The term ''diphthong'' comes from the Greek ''di'' meaning two, and ''phthongos'' meaning sound. Identifying diphthongs and understanding how they are pronounced is an important part of learning and understanding English grammar and phonology.

What Are Vowels?

Vowel sounds are pronounced in different parts of the vocal tract

Vowels are letters in English that are formed with an open vocal structure. Typically, they are also the best clues in English as to when a new syllable is starting: one vowel sound per syllable is normal. Vowel diphthongs, however, put two vowel sounds together in a single syllable. The English letters that correspond to vowel sounds are A, E, I, O, U, and in some cases Y. Diphthongs are sometimes called ''gliding vowels'' because they are made up of two vowel sounds that glide together when pronounced.

Short Vowels

In English, vowels are usually split into the categories of short and long based on their sound. These sounds can best be explained with the use of symbols that were developed for the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA) to describe language sounds with the greatest possible degree of accuracy. Individual short vowels, not combined into diphthongs, include the following:

/æ/ short A sound cat, apple
/ɛ/ short E sound pet, empty
/ɪ/ short I sound kiss, insect
/ɒ/ short O sound got, ostrich
/ʌ/ short U sound pun, under
/ʊ/ short U sound put, good
/ə/ unstressed E sound about, police

These vowel sounds appear in many words in English and are important to recognize. There are other, less common vowel sounds in English as well, but this list will cover the vowels found in the majority of words.

Long Vowels

There are many long vowels that are actually diphthongs, but there are also a few that are pronounced alone. Long vowels are sounds that are usually stressed in a given word and have a more drawn-out pronunciation than their short counterparts. English long vowels that are not diphthongs include:

/i:/ long EE sound keep, lady
/u:/ long OO sound food, glue

All other long vowels in English are actually combinations of vowels that are pronounced as diphthongs.

Diphthong Examples

This chart provides information on where and how each vowel is pronounced

Diphthongs are identifiable because they start in one place in the vocal tract and end somewhere else. The way that the sound travels between the two sounds is what produces the gliding effect associated with diphthongs. The following diphthongs list includes the most common diphthongs in English. However, it is important to note that not everyone who speaks English will pronounce all of these diphthongs according to the examples given. There are many different varieties of English. These examples are based on how they are pronounced in Standard American English.

/aʊ/

When someone stubs their toe, they might be quite likely to say ''Ow!'' This is a great example of the /aʊ/ diphthong in action. The /aʊ/ begins with an open, unrounded sound pronounced in the middle of the mouth. It then becomes rounded and closed, and travels far back in the mouth. Words that use the /aʊ/ diphthong include:

  • Mouth
  • Cow
  • Sound
  • Out

When saying this diphthong, many people will be able to see and feel their lips close to produce a smaller embouchure at the end of the sound.

/aɪ/

What do life, behind, quite, and I'm have in common? They all use the /aɪ/ diphthong in Standard American English. This diphthong begins the same way as the /aʊ/ diphthong, with an open and unrounded sound. Instead of moving to the back of the mouth, it moves forward and closes. The sound remains unrounded throughout the pronunciation of the diphthong.

/eɪ/

If you ask to pay on a date, you will use the /eɪ/ diphthong. Words that use this diphthong are numerous in English and include:

  • Say
  • Freight
  • Wave
  • They

Unlike the other diphthongs described so far, this one involves very little movement in the mouth. The first sound is fronted, unrounded, and somewhat closed. The second sound is pronounced just slightly further back and is more closed. This diphthong is often particularly challenging for second-language speakers of English, partly because the movement of the sound is so slight.

/eə/

The /eə/ diphthong can be difficult to define because it is also quite a subtle sound change. There is also a lot of variation in how Americans use this diphthong. Words that feature /eə/ include:

  • Square
  • Air
  • Their
  • Where

One reason why this diphthong is hard to describe is because the vowels involved are both in the middle of the vowel chart and do not have particularly clearly defined features. The vowel starts moderately closed and fronted and then gets more central and open, remaining unrounded throughout.

/ɪə/

The /ɪə/ exists in American English, but it is often conflated with /i:/. Words that include the /ɪə/ diphthong in some dialects are:

  • Appear
  • Beer
  • Period
  • Here

The first sound is a fairly closed, unrounded, and fronted vowel. The sound then drops back to the center of the vocal space and gets somewhat more open. It remains unrounded. It is possible to feel one's jaw open slightly when pronouncing the second vowel in this diphthong.

/oʊ/

This diphthong is best demonstrated by the word ''Oh!'' It sounds just like the letter O. Words that feature this sound include:

  • Over
  • Go
  • Trope
  • Growing

Unlike most of the other diphthongs on this list, the first sound in this diphthong starts right at the back of the vocal space. It is a moderately closed and rounded sound. The second sound moves forward just slightly in the vocal space and closes a little but remains rounded.

/ɔɪ/

/ɔɪ/ may be the diphthong with the greatest degree of vowel movement in English, at least in Standard American English. It can be found in words like:

  • Boy
  • Oil
  • Destroy
  • Choice

The first sound in this diphthong is an open, rounded, and backed vowel. It then travels all the way to the near-front of the mouth, becomes unrounded, and closes significantly. This means that all of the basic components of the vowel are flipped in this diphthong.

/ʊə/

The /ʊə/ diphthong is the final common English diphthong on this list, but it is not the last one that exists. This diphthong can be found in the following words:

  • Jury
  • Pure
  • Ensure
  • Duel

Like several other diphthongs, this one is tricky to explain because of the placement of the vowels within the vocal space. The first vowel is a rounded, closed, and backed vowel. It then moves to become an unrounded vowel right in the center of the vocal space that is not particularly open or closed.

Tips for Learning English Diphthongs

Getting the hang of diphthongs in English, either as a second-language speaker or as a linguistics or grammar student, takes practice. One great way to improve one's understanding of diphthongs is to come to grips with the IPA symbols. These symbols help identify specific vowels that are not always represented by specific letters in English. Practicing saying different diphthongs with particular attention to how one's mouth moves is also helpful.

Diphthongs in Dialects

Standard American English is just one of many dialects of English. Many people will not pronounce the words listed here with these particular diphthongs. For some speakers of American English and most speakers of Canadian English, for instance, there is a phenomenon that enters the dialect that is called raising. This means that the vowels in words like ''sight'' and ''side'' are pronounced differently, with only the latter word using the /aɪ/ diphthong. Other dialects of American English pronounce words like ''life'' using /a/, without pronouncing the second vowel of the diphthong. This can be found in some Southern accents and is a process called monophthongization.

Diphthong vs Digraph

''Digraph'' is an orthographic term for two letters that are pronounced as one sound. Examples of digraphs include ''ch'' and ''th'' in English. Some vowels (not diphthongs) are also represented using digraphs, like the ''ue'' in words like ''true.'' Digraphs and diphthongs are not necessarily connected, but it is important to understand the diphthong vs digraph concept to avoid confusion.

Lesson Summary

A diphthong is a sound composed of two vowel sounds in English, from the Greek for ''two sounds.'' While a syllable is usually defined as a group of letters with one distinct vowel sound, a diphthong does not create two different syllables. Common diphthongs in English include:

  • /aʊ/
  • /aɪ/
  • /eɪ/
  • /eə/
  • /ɪə/
  • /oʊ/
  • /ɔɪ/
  • /ʊə/

The International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA) provides an effective way of describing these sounds.

What Is a Syllable?

Sometimes, non-native speakers have difficulties with saying or identifying diphthongs, but with a bit of practice at counting syllables and listening for vowel sounds in words, anyone can identify a diphthong! In order to understand what a diphthong is, it is important to understand what a syllable is and to know how syllables are formed.

Syllables can be defined as a single letter or a group of letters that include a distinct vowel sound. For example, consider the word water:

In this word, there are two syllables: ''wa'' and ''ter.'' Each group has its own specific vowel sound, in this case the ''ah'' sound in ''wa'' and the ''eh'' sound in ''er.''

For another example, look at the word blackberry :

  • There are three syllables here: ''black'', ''ber'', and ''ry''. There's the ''a'' sound in ''black'', the ''eh'' sound in ''ber,'' and the hard ''ee'' in ''ry,'' created by the letter ''y.''

Typically, if you want to check how many syllables a word has, you can say it out loud and clap each time that you may say another distinctive vowel sound. Try it with the example words: water - hear the two syllables? Blackberry: this word has three syllables.

What Is a Diphthong?

A diphthong is a sound that is made up of two separate vowel sounds within the same syllable. The word is derived from Greek for ''having two sounds.'' If we go back to our syllable examples, you may notice that each syllable only has one distinctive vowel sound within it.

However, there are some syllables that have two distinctive vowel sounds within them. For example, the word sound. This word only has one syllable. However, that syllable has two vowel sounds that run together, and each sound is just a bit different. There is the ''ow'' vowel sound that the ''o'' makes, and that sound turns into something of an ''ooh'' sound made by the ''u''. Sound…Sound…hear it?

Let's take another word with a diphthong in it:

The word coin has a diphthong, too. Coin…we can hear that it only has only syllable in it, but that single syllable has two vowel sounds within it. The ''o'' makes a sort of ''oy'' sound, and the ''i'' makes an ''oi'' sound. Coin…coin. Again, this vowel sound runs together as one unified sound that changes in pronunciation.

Diphthongs can be tricky to master for non-native English speakers. If you are asked to identify a diphthong, it is helpful to make sure that you can identify the syllables in a word and note how your pronunciation might change if you are pronouncing a longer vowel sound within a syllable.

Diphthong Examples

In this table we find some more examples of diphthong sounds. Long vowels are pronounced exactly as the letter is pronounced. Each diphthong has two sounds: the first one of which blends into the second one.

Diphthong Sound Example Words
Long A, long E Day, made
Long A, ''ur '' sound Fair, chair
Long E, ''ur '' soundDeer, fear
Long I, long E Pie, slide
Long O, long E Toy, coil
Long O, ''oo'' sound Toe, float
Long U, ''ur '' sound Pure, sure

Lesson Summary

Diphthongs occur in syllables that contain two distinctive vowel sounds. Syllables are groups of letters within a word that have at least one vowel sound in them. By taking note of the number of syllables in a word and by paying attention to the way that you might change your pronunciation on longer vowel sounds within a syllable while speaking that syllable out loud, you may be more easily able to identify diphthongs.

Diphthong Practice Activities

Identify Diphthongs

For this first activity, you will review a list of words and identify any words that contain diphthongs. Simply highlight or underline the diphthong in the word. Keep in mind that not every word will contain a diphthong. After you have completed Activity A, complete the following writing activity.

Activity A

  1. Hammer
  2. Under
  3. Choice
  4. Europe
  5. Tooth
  6. Horrible
  7. Stairs
  8. Around
  9. Panda
  10. Manicure
  11. Employ
  12. Alarm
  13. Goose
  14. Paint
  15. Toast

Activity B

For the second activity, write about the last vacation you had. Write 1-2 paragraphs about this vacation. Once you are done writing, identify all of the diphthongs present in the words you used. To do this, simply underline the vowels that create the diphthong.

Answers and Examples:

Activity A:

Choice, Europe, Stairs, Around, Manicure, Employ, Paint, and Toast all contain diphthongs.

Activity B:

You may use the following paragraph for Activity B if you do not wish to write your own. For example, you will see that the word coast has a diphthong. Identify the other diphthongs in this paragraph.

This past summer, I traveled to Costa Rica in Central America. I had never been to a Spanish-speaking country, so I was excited to practice my Spanish-speaking skills. When we landed in San Jose, I was immediately engrossed in the culture. While in this amazing country, I ziplined, rafted down a river, snorkeled, kayaked by a volcano, played sand volleyball on the coast, explored national parks, learned about sustainability, and ate a lot of rice, beans, and pineapple. I was also able to speak some Spanish with friendly locals.
