Do you want to know if your students in an English Language Development program are able to utilize the language without difficulty at all times? This lesson discusses how you can evaluate the program.

Why Evaluate?

Let's explore the English Language Development (ELD) program at G.O.O.D School. Here, English learners perform fairly well in the academic aspect. However, English learners have difficulty with daily life conversations. In other words, learners do not seem to have sociolinguistic competence. This is the ability to communicate appropriately by using the right words and expressions in a variety of topics and settings. Luckily, there is program evaluation. This is the process that assesses whether a program is or is not accomplishing its initial goals. From this definition, we can deduce the reason why program evaluation is necessary. It not only helps determine if a program is effective, but also determines what modifications or changes are needed. Now, let's learn how to evaluate.

How to Evaluate?

First, you need a team in charge of the evaluation. Each organization determines the number of people in the team and how to form it. For instance, at G.O.O.D School, the principal and the ELD teachers had a meeting and formed an evaluation team. The team includes the ELD program coordinator and four teachers with a lot of experience with sociolinguistic competence. In each step to evaluate the ELD program, the team is going to ask themselves some questions. Let's start!

Goals are First

While there is not a single formula for program evaluation, it is first important to review the goals or objective of the program. The evaluation team can ask themselves these questions:

  • Are the objectives of the program in place as expected?
  • What specific data shows that the objectives or goals are in place?

Let's illustrate with G.O.O.D School. One of its objectives is 'To develop in students sociolinguistic competence so they are able to use the language not only in academic contexts but in a variety of social situations.' Teachers discuss this. For example, a teacher says that she has seen students struggle when they have to explain what happened in a given situation. Another teacher says that parents complain their children cannot perform out of school in social situations that involve English. Finally, the coordinator highlights the low scores in tests that require students to talk about different topics. In short, goals are not in place. Let's move on!

Evaluate Aspects of the Program

Once it is clear that goals or objectives are not in place, there is further work to do. Next is to analyze the key aspects of the program. When it comes to ELD programs, three key aspects include materials, assessments, and related instructional components.

Materials

Regarding materials, the question your team and you need to ask is:

  • Do the teaching materials promote sociolinguistic competence?

When materials promote sociolinguistic competence, they pose challenges for students that have to do with using the language in a variety of situations and topics.

Let's look at G.O.O.D School. The materials that teachers use within the ELD program focus solely on vocabulary and grammar with commands like 'Fill in the blanks with the right word' or 'Write sentences in the simple past.' While this builds on the mechanics aspect of language, this does not lead students to being competent communicators in a variety of settings. Thus, the team at G.O.O.D School concludes that the materials need modification.

To solve materials issues, there are two options. First, purchase extra materials that focus on sociolinguistic competence. Also, there are free materials online teachers that can use. Second, replace all the existing materials with new ones that focus on several language skills, including sociolinguistic competence.

Assessments

One thing that teachers may have to consider is that they may need to prepare their students for law-mandated assessments (e.g. annual standardized tests). But the good news is that teachers can usually control the assessments they design for the ELD program, including homework assignments and quizzes. In that way, teachers can assess students in a way that matches the materials used throughout the academic year or semester.

The question to ask is:

  • Do the assessments in the ELD program get students using the language in a way that mimics a variety of social situations and topics?

If the answer is 'No,' then the team can work to modify the assessments and include the type that promotes sociolinguistic competence.

To illustrate, at G.O.O.D School, teachers assess students similarly to the practice sections in the teaching materials. The assessments include commands like 'Fill in the blanks' and 'Underline the adjectives.' From now on, teachers at G.O.O.D School will give assessments like simulated conversation practice, presentations on a given topic, etc.

Instructional Components

To put your teachers at ease during program evaluation, it is a good idea to clarify to all that instructional skills will be analyzed in order to improve. This is necessary because, at times, teachers might feel that their job stability is at risk. However, instructional components of an ELD program do need evaluation. The question to ask is:

  • Does the instruction ELD teachers apply in the classroom include activities that promote sociolinguistic competence?

The answer is not difficult because activities that build sociolinguistic competence include practice that gets students to use the language in diverse contexts and topics. Examples of activities are: 'Talk to your neighbor and find out what s/he would change about the community' or 'What would you say in this situation?'

The best way to improve instructional components of the ELD program is training teachers so they include more activities that promote sociolinguistic competence.

Lesson Summary

Sociolinguistic competence is the ability to communicate appropriately by using the right words and expressions in a variety of topics and settings. To determine if an ELD program has this aspect, there is program evaluation, which is the process that allows to assess whether a program is or not accomplishing its initial goals. Schools need to form an evaluation team who would then analyze if the goals of the ELD program are in place. If not, then the team can separately analyze key components of the ELD program. The components include materials, assessments, and instructional practices.
