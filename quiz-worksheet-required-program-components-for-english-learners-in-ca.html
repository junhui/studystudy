Mario and Diana are two Mexican parents who are granted a waiver in their child's school in California so that their child is excluded from ELD instruction. This means that the child is going to receive:

  1. L1 instruction

  2. ELA classes in two languages

  3. math classes in two languages

  4. ELA sessions in English

As per Chapter 7 in California's RLA Framework, Universal Access enables English learners to:

  1. attend bilingual classes

  2. attend language arts classes along with English speakers

  3. attend language arts classes in their primary language

  4. attend language development classes to learn English

The English Language Proficiency Assessments for California (ELPAC) allows teachers to determine the following regarding English learners:

  1. if the student speaks a primary language other than English

  2. the student's language proficiency level

  3. the students' grade level

  4. if they had previous formal schooling

The ELD/ELA Framework in California defines standards for these areas:

  1. English language development and science

  2. English language development and math

  3. history/math in English

  4. English language development and English language arts

Anita is an English learner in California. She receives ELD instruction, which would ideally lead her to be comfortable in social situations but also in:

  1. math classes in English

  2. conversations with native speakers

  3. academic settings in English

  4. academic settings in her primary language
