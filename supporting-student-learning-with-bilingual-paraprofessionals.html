Bilingual paraprofessionals have a very important role in the context of ELD and SDAIE programs. In this lesson, we have an overview of those roles as well as a brief discussion on the best practices for bilingual paraprofessionals.

What is a Bilingual Paraprofessional?

Bilingual paraprofessionals are certified to collaborate with teachers by using mainly their knowledge of two languages. As you probably think, very often bilingual paraprofessionals speak both English and Spanish. This is the most common language pair that paraprofessionals speak but since schools across the US have a wide range of foreign students, there is need for other bilingual paraprofessionals. For instance, some schools might need English-Chinese, English-Arabic, and other paraprofessionals.

Now, let's take a look at what the role of bilingual paraprofessionals is in the classroom and, at the same time, the best practices for each role.

Roles of Bilingual Paraprofessionals

The knowledge that bilingual paraprofessionals have of another language aside from English, comes in handy particularly in the context of English Language Development (ELD) and Specially Designed Academic Instruction in English (SDAIE) programs. Let's clarify these two types of programs.

In an ELD program, classrooms are for students who are learning English and the lessons are specifically designed to develop English language skills (reading, writing, speaking, and listening). In a SDAIE program, the lessons are designed to teach curricular content in English in subjects such as literature, math, science, etc. to students who are still in the process of learning English.

Now, let's put these programs in perspective by meeting Clara. She is a Spanish-speaking student who is in the ELD program at her school as she came from Colombia and is learning the English language. At the same time, she is in a SDAIE program because she is in third grade in an English-speaking school and needs to learn math, science, and other subjects in English. We can imagine how overwhelming it can be to learn a new language and, on top of it, to learn academic content in the new language. This is precisely when bilingual paraprofessionals come in handy. Let's look at their roles now.

Collaboration Role with the Teacher

Bilingual paraprofessionals are primarily present in ELD and SDAIE classrooms in order to collaborate with the teacher in charge of the classrooms. While this is not work directly with the students, it does support their learning because the bilingual teacher can give input considering the needs of bilingual students. Collaborating with a teacher means that the bilingual paraprofessionals would help the teacher to prepare lessons, grade school work, do administrative tasks as directed by the teacher (such as creating folders, preparing letters to parents etc.).

In this aspect, bilingual paraprofessionals work along with the teacher, who directs them in terms of what needs to get done. Regarding the best practice when collaborating with a teacher, it is important that bilingual paraprofessionals remember they are in the classroom to work along with a teacher by assisting them. Thus, in case a bilingual paraprofessional is not sure what to do, the ethical approach is to ask the teacher for direction or wait for direction rather than taking over the duties that are mainly for the classroom teacher.

Individual Assistance Role to Students

Bilingual paraprofessionals are in the classroom to assist individual students during ELD and SDAIE classrooms by making sure students understand the academic content as well as the tasks the teacher assigns. For instance, the bilingual paraprofessional who works in Clara's class approaches Clara during a reading comprehension task because Clara struggles with the new vocabulary. The bilingual professional slowly speaks in English to Clara and gives her a definition of 'barn' plus an example of a sentence that includes the word. Clara is still not sure but asks if 'barn' means 'granero'. Because the bilingual paraprofessional speaks Spanish, she confirms to Clara she is correct. Similarly, during a SDAIE lesson for math, Clara is confused about the steps to make an equation. In this case, the bilingual paraprofessional speaks in Spanish to Clara to explain the process and, then, repeats the instructions in English. This helps Clara clarify how to make an equation.

In this case, we can see the bilingual paraprofessional can help by individually speaking to a student in English or Spanish as needed. However, the best practice bilingual paraprofessionals should apply is to use English mainly while Spanish is a tool to help the student. The reason for this is that students in ELD and SDAIE programs need to focus on developing their English skills.

Translation and Interpretation Role

The fact bilingual paraprofessionals speak two languages at least means that they are a great asset not just for the students but also for the school as a whole. Very often, schools need bilingual paraprofessionals to use their language skills to translate not only in the classroom but for parents and teachers during school events, parent evenings, or other events. This is to facilitate communication between teachers and parents who do not speak English fluently. This is an indirect way to support students' learning because it allows clear communication between the people involved in a child's education.

Also, the school might get bilingual paraprofessionals to translate letters or emails going out to parents or vice versa if they are not in English.

Regarding translation and interpretation, the best practice is to carefully translate/interpret to make sure that the nuances of either language do not get lost in translation or misinterpreted by either party. As we know, translation and interpretation is a whole field of specialization and a bilingual paraprofessional can certainly further their career if they take a certification course in this area.

Lesson Summary

Bilingual paraprofessionals are certified to collaborate with classroom teachers by using mainly their knowledge of two languages. These paraprofessionals are a great resource in the context of English Language Development (ELD) and Specially Designed Academic Instruction in English (SDAIE) programs. Their roles include collaboration with a teacher with lessons, grading, administrative work (in either case, the best practice is to work along and under the direction of the teacher). Another role is the one to individually assist students in the classroom (by helping in English or the native language the student speaks). Finally, bilingual paraprofessionals often play the role of translation/interpretation to facilitate communication between parents and the school.
