Anne's students often pronounce some words incorrectly. To help students correct this, Anne can teach students how to:

  1. play-role

  2. self-monitor

  3. request for clarification

  4. practice repetition of the mispronounced words

Anne wants her students to talk about what they like best about school. While most students get talking about it, there is one who just responds 'break time.' How can Anne get the student to elaborate?

  1. by asking him lots of questions

  2. by asking the student what else he likes

  3. by asking the student to say a full sentence

  4. by getting the student to talk to a peer

Anne's language learners struggle with the pattern for stating sentences in the present continuous. Sometimes they forget the verb 'to be,' other times they do not add 'ing.' Anne can help students with this pattern by teaching how to practice:

  1. repetition

  2. elaboration

  3. formulaic expressions

  4. appeals for assistance

Anne speaks French and as she sees one of her French students struggle with getting the use and meaning of 'thus,' she speaks French to the student. This is a strategy called:

  1. code-switching

  2. self-monitoring

  3. role-play

  4. elaboration

Anne would like her students to learn the use of formulaic expressions. So these expressions really stick with students, she should give students the following:

  1. a guide to how to practice repetition

  2. an opportunity to elaborate

  3. an opportunity to assist her in class

  4. a context
