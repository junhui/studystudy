What is an English Language Proficiency Assessment?

Assessments are essential in education, particularly for English Language Learners (ELLs) or emergent bilingual students who are acquiring a second language. Educators have developed various methods and tools to evaluate the language skills and abilities of non-native English speakers. Language proficiency assessments are a common type of assessment that focuses on the level of proficiency a student has in a particular language. These types of assessments are critical for educators to gauge the progress of their ELL students and provide them with targeted support to enhance their language skills.

However, how ELL students view these assessments can differ significantly in perception. Some ELL students see language proficiency assessments as a means of achieving a score rather than an opportunity to learn and improve overall language skills. They focus on achieving a high score due to the impact these assessments can have on their future academic and professional opportunities. In contrast, others might focus on how the assessment feedback highlights strengths and weaknesses in their language skills.

Therefore, the design and administration of English proficiency assessments must be sensitive to the unique perspectives and challenges ELL students face. Educators must focus on providing clear instructions and feedback and offering support and accommodations for students who require them, ensuring the assessments are culturally and linguistically appropriate for all students. By adopting a student-centered approach to language assessment, educators can help ELL students develop a positive attitude toward assessments and see them as valuable tools for developing language skills.

Purpose of Assessing English Language Learners

The primary purpose of assessing English Language Learners is to determine their language proficiency, set educational goals, and monitor progress toward those goals. More specifically, language assessments provide insights and data in the following areas.

  • Identifying strengths and weaknesses in language skills: Proficiency assessments determine student abilities in listening, speaking, reading, and writing skills. This data can provide educators with a baseline to inform instruction and set academic milestones.
  • Classifying ELL students into appropriate proficiency levels: Assessment data can place a student at the appropriate ability level, such as beginning, early intermediate, intermediate, early advanced, and advanced. This allows educators to accurately place ELL students in the proper language courses and programs.
  • Monitoring language development progress: By conducting regular assessments, teachers can track student progress, identify areas of need and provide students with consistent feedback on their language development.
  • Lesson planning: By analyzing assessment data, educators can identify patterns and trends in student performance, which can inform lesson planning allowing them to focus lesson design on relevant components.
  • Compliance with the Elementary and Secondary Education Act (ESEA): This legislation requires educators to assess the English proficiency of all ELL students, then use the results to determine student eligibility for langue support and services.

The Elementary and Secondary Education Act (ESEA) requires all students be assessed for English proficiency.

Ways to Assess English Language Proficiency

There are various means of assessing student language proficiency, and educators need to be aware of these methods to evaluate their students' language skills accurately. One form of assessment is standardized tests such as the International English Language Testing System (IELTS) and the Test of English as a Foreign Languages (TOEFL), commonly used to evaluate international students. Tests such as the WIDA and English Language Proficiency Assessments for California (ELPAC) are standardized language proficiency tests commonly used to assess ELL students in U.S. public school systems. These tests measure reading, writing, speaking, and listening skills and provide a standardized score that assesses overall English language proficiency.

Additional means of assessing ELL students are teacher observations and evaluations. Teachers can use informal assessments such as classroom observations, teacher-made tests, and language portfolios to evaluate the proficiency of kids in their classes. These forms of assessment provide a more comprehensive picture of a student's language ability, considering fluency, pronunciation, and overall communication skills. Additionally, Home Language Surveys (HLS)are questionnaires sent home for parents and guardians to help identify potential ELL students.

It is important to note that while formal language exams like the IELTS or TOEFL are helpful, they may not be appropriate for all students. Younger students not preparing for these official exams will require teachers to focus on reinforcing and summarizing classroom content rather than preparing them for exams. Other assessment forms can be used with younger learners to help them review and remember classroom content. Different assessments helpful in assessing English language proficiency include written, oral, and general proficiency tests.

Writing Tests

Written assessments allow teachers to assess student analytical skills along with language development skills.

Written language tests can evaluate language development for various formative and summative reasons comprehensively and objectively. This approach can help teachers identify areas where students need improvement and tailor their instruction. Written assessments appear in different forms. Moreover, these types of assessments can also provide educators with a look at student ability to express their thoughts and ideas.

  • Open-ended long-answer questions: These require students to write detailed responses to specific prompts.
  • Essays: Longform writing like essays provide students with an opportunity to demonstrate specific writing skills like organization, coherence, and clarity of expression.
  • Reading response questions: Using these questions helps evaluate student comprehension of written texts.
  • Editing tasks: Editing work requires students to identify and correct errors in written texts by assessing grammar and syntax skills.

Choosing which form of written assessment an instructor uses depends on the specific goal or skill they are evaluating. For example, essay questions could be more appropriate when assessing student ability to express complex ideas in writing, while editing tasks evaluate grammatical knowledge. Further, reading response answers evaluate comprehension and ability to analyze key information by writing.

Oral Tests

Oral tests assess ELL student ability to communicate effectively in spoken English. They evaluate student ability to express thoughts and ideas, respond to questions, and engage in conversation using appropriate grammar, syntax, and vocabulary. Oral assessments can take many forms:

  • Individual speaking tests or Oral Proficiency Interviews (OPI): These require students to speak on a specific topic or answer questions the examiner poses. They evaluate student ability to speak fluently, accurately, and appropriately. Additionally, teachers can evaluate pronunciation, intonation, and overall speaking proficiency.
  • Group discussions: Working in groups places students in a social context and assesses their ability to use language to interact with others. Teachers can evaluate student ability to listen and express ideas coherently and ''on the fly'' in a more real-world setting. Further, ELL students can learn from native speakers.

These types of assessments provide a more authentic assessment of student language abilities and knowledge, as well as provide immediate feedback and allow teachers to identify areas of need and adjust teaching strategies accordingly.

Proficiency Tests

As mentioned, English language proficiency tests (ELP) are important in second language evaluation to assess language proficiency. The TOEFL and the IELTS are two common tests to evaluate student ability to use English in an academic or professional context. Universities and employers often use these tests to determine language proficiency levels.

Both the TOEFL and IELTS have four main components: reading, listening, speaking, and writing. The reading and listening components are multiple-choice, while the speaking and writing components require test-takers to demonstrate their abilities to communicate effectively in English. However, it is crucial that teachers do not ''teach to the test'' in an attempt to prepare students. Research shows this is ineffective for language development because it does not help students retain and use the knowledge and skills they've gained. Instead, teachers should use a more comprehensive approach, focusing on helping ELL students gain communication skills, not just the limited knowledge needed to score well on a standardized test.

Alternative Assessments for English Language Learners

English Language Learners may focus too much on achieving a high score on English Proficiency Assessments and forget to measure their progress.

In addition to traditional written and oral assessments, many alternative assessment methods can provide valuable insights into the language proficiency of English language learners. These alternative methods can provide a more holistic understanding of language proficiency by considering different learning styles, backgrounds, and experiences.

  • Portfolios: Portfolios are a collection of student work over time, including assignments, presentations, and other projects demonstrating language proficiency. They provide a broader view of student progress over time.
  • Informal communication: This approach involves observing student communication in a causal setting, for example, during group work or class-wide discussions. This can provide insights into how a student uses language naturally and spontaneously without the pressure of a formal assessment.
  • Oral presentations or performances: Activities like role-playing, interviews, plays, and acting-out scenes can reduce test anxiety and allow ELL students to demonstrate skills more proficiently.
  • Project-based assessments: Students complete projects requiring English language skills. Examples may include creating videos or presenting a research paper or posters. A key component is the use of rubrics or performance criteria, which has the benefit of assessing academic progress as well as language skills. Additionally, this type of assessment can evaluate higher-order thinking skills.
  • Non-verbal assessment: Best utilized for ELL students who are not proficient yet in English. This method allows teachers to observe student body language, gestures, and other non-verbal cues to assess their understanding and communication abilities.
  • Online and technology-based: Mobile apps can be beneficial in providing interactive assessments with feedback in real time.

Alternative assessment methods for ELL students can provide valuable insight into language proficiency. Educators can gain a more accurate picture of student language development.

Lesson Summary

Language proficiency assessments are an important component in education that determine the proficiency level and ability of English Language Learners (ELLs), who are students acquiring English as a second language. There are many types of English language proficiency tests (ELP):

  • Standardized Tests: These include the International English Language Testing System (IELTS) and the Test of English as a Foreign Language (TOEFL) which measure skills in reading, writing, speaking, and listening and provide a standardized score.
  • Written Tests: For example, open-ended long answers or essays evaluate ELL students' proficiency in writing and skills like organization, coherency, and complexity of expression.
  • Oral Tests: These assess spoken language skills such as fluency, pronunciation, intonation, and overall speaking proficiency.
  • Alternative Assessments: This includes portfolios, project-based assessments, non-verbal assessments, and home language surveys (HLS) that look at language development from a more holistic approach.

ELL students sometimes view language assessments in terms of a score they must obtain, forgetting the importance of measuring progress toward the goal of language development. Educators should work to counter this outlook by not ''teaching to the test.'' Instead, teachers need to take a more holistic approach that focuses on developing skills and noting improvements and areas of need. This is particularly important for younger students not yet preparing for standardized tests like the IELTS or TOEFL. Teachers should focus on reinforcing and summarizing classroom content for these younger students and reminding all their ELL students that the goal is authentic communication.

Why Assessments Matter

As most educators know, some students excel at assessments while others dread the very mention of a test. No matter how students feel about assessments, they are pretty much universally required, at least to some degree, in almost every formal academic setting. The way in which you use assessments can have a significant impact on both their effectiveness and usefulness for identifying student needs and helping students progress.

English learners often approach assessments - ways to gauge their language proficiency as well as academic content knowledge - a bit differently than native speakers. First of all, it is not uncommon for English language learners (ELLs) to approach assessments as purely a needed score rather than an opportunity to learn. Because of this, some learners don't understand the importance of retaining language information beyond the assessment. This pitfall can be avoided by constantly reminding, and showing, students that the English knowledge they are gaining needs to be employed well beyond an assessment, and that this knowledge is necessary for successful communication both inside and outside of the classroom.

The Usefulness of Assessments

Assessments serve several functions for English learners. The primary functions include:

To assess language level and ability: Some programs require individuals to take an official English language exam before entering into an academic program. Two of the most common English assessments are IELTS (International English Language Testing System) and TOEFL (Test of English as a Foreign Language). These exams provide students with a language score in four areas:

  • Speaking
  • Reading
  • Writing
  • Listening

The higher the score a student can obtain, the better chance he or she may have for entry into an academic program.

To assess a student's current abilities and strengths, while at the some time identifying possible weaknesses and potential difficulties: Some ELLs may excel at English speaking, but struggle with writing and vice versa. Properly administered assessments can help teachers realize which areas need to be reinforced in order to prepare a student for success.

To make placement and evaluation decisions: Many English-based academic programs will give assessments to their students periodically in order to evaluate student work and progress and determine appropriate class level placement.

Planning and Placement

Assessments are useful for identifying specific programs and classes from which students may benefit. If your program allows the flexibility, assessments can show which students should move up or down in terms of class level. Some institutions, however, place students based on age rather than English language ability. If this is the case, delivering content that is appropriately challenging for all students may be difficult, but not impossible.

If you find that the levels of your students in the same class vary significantly, try to use assessments that allow for a range of responses. The following are examples of how assessments can allow students to demonstrate different levels of ability in terms of vocabulary and grammar.

Open-ended long answer, or essay, questions: A range of writing prompts and questions allow for both simple and complex responses.

  • Describe an important event in your life.
  • Write about a person who has helped you.
  • What are your favorite and least favorite parts of learning English?
  • How will understanding English help you in your life?

Individual speaking interviews and small group discussions: If possible, try to set aside time to speak with students individually. During this one-on-one time you can:

  • Review content that has been covered in class.
  • Ask students about how they feel about the information they are learning.
  • Use constructive criticism to identify areas in which students need to improve.
  • Develop individual study plans and tailor exercises or assignments to meet student needs.
  • Provide encouragement and a clear vision for academic goals and milestones.

Assessments can also be a useful resource for lesson planning. For instance, if your learners are studying for a specific assessment, such as an SAT or ACT exam, you can provide them with practice materials, classroom exercises, and homework assignments that specifically address the types of questions on these exams. It's important, however, not to only teach to the test. You'll want to provide plenty of realistic practice opportunities, as students will need to call upon the knowledge they've acquired well after completing an assessment.

This is also the case as students enter higher education. An acceptable SAT and IELTS score may be required for a non-native English speaking student to gain entry into a college or university, but getting an acceptable score is just the first step in the process. Students must continue to learn and develop their language skills if they wish to be successful in a higher education setting.

If you are teaching younger students and don't have the added pressure of preparing them for advanced exams, you'll want to use assessments that reinforce and summarize classroom content. One of your primary goals should be to keep students motivated, even if they sometimes struggle or falter on quizzes or exams.

Lesson Summary

While some English assessments (IELTS, TOEFL, SAT) are well-established exams that are sometimes required by educational institutions, it may be necessary for you to create your own assessments in order to meet the needs of your learners. Assessments can be used to determine a student's language abilities and where he or she should be placed, but assessments are also useful for keeping students engaged with the English language. When students are aware that assessments are a part of the learning process and not the end, they'll be more likely to understand the importance of carrying that knowledge forward.

Frequently Asked Questions

What type of assessment is used to identify an English language learner?

There are many different assessments used to identify English Language Learners (ELL). Some common types of assessments include language proficiency assessments like the WIDA, the California English Language Development Test (CELDT), and the English Language Proficiency Test (ELPT). Additionally, there are diagnostic, academic (SAT/ACT), and classroom assessments that can also be used to identify ELLs.

What are language proficiency assessments?

Language proficiency assessments are standardized tests that measure a student's ability to understand, speak, read, and write in a particular language. These types of assessments provide a comprehensive picture of student proficiency.
