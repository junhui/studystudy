Multiculturalism

The United States of America is made up of a multitude of interesting and different people, all of whom bring something new and distinct to the table. From a big city's bustling China Town to a small town's little diner, every corner of this country is filled with the individual cultures that make up this nation.

This concept is referred to as multiculturalism: the existence of many different cultures within one set group. When walking down the street of New York City, one might hear several languages being spoken around them at the same time. When deciding what to eat at the grocery store, one might find they have trouble choosing between the sushi from Japan or the fried chicken from down South. When deciding where to go on a Sunday morning, one might choose the mosque, the Baptist church, or to sit at home on their couch. These are all examples of a multicultural society.

Similarly, the concept of multiculturalism exists within the study of literary works or as a part of literature studies. Multicultural literature is important for several reasons: it can be seen as an instructional device used to open the minds of students, it assists with understanding diversity in the classroom and the world as a whole, and it helps students build respect for people of different cultures and lifestyles.

Multicultural Literature

There is a reason many teachers choose to have their students read books and short stories from authors with a variety of backgrounds; it fosters the understanding and acceptance of multicultural literature in a way reading a '"normal"' school-related text might not. For example, when reading The Crucible by Arthur Miller, students learn about the lives of townsmen and townswomen from New England in the early 1690s. This brings value to students and scholars because it helps them to understand what life was like during that time.

However, it does not show the variety of life that exists in today's day and age like multicultural literature might. Texts like The Kite Runner by Khaled Hosseini and One Hundred Years of Solitude by Gabriel Garcia Marquez teach about the lives of people in Afghanistan and Colombia, respectively. The Kite Runner teaches readers the importance of friendship and how it extends well beyond social class, while One Hundred Years of Solitude shows the reader how families of other cultures sometimes attempt to hold on to their true traditions even in the face of change. This is relevant to readers of all ages because they may never get to see with their own eyes what the culture is like there; reading about it allows them to be transported and open their minds to new concepts they may never have considered before.

Multicultural literature is especially important when it comes to battling negative stereotypes that have perhaps lived in young people's minds before they allowed themselves to learn about different societies. A stereotype is a negatively held belief by people of a common group that shows a prejudiced attitude, critical judgment, or pessimistic opinion about another group. It is important for people of all ages to battle negative stereotypes, and the easiest way to do so is by learning a bit more about the people one is critically judging.

Importance of Multicultural Literature

Readers of all ages may gain an appreciation of other people's cultures and beliefs through consuming multicultural literature. American society has steadily grown and changed over the last few decades, with people from all over the globe immigrating here to start a new life and experience the American Dream. International travel and access to social and electronic media have allowed people to gain acceptance and a greater understanding of cultures apart from their own, but there is still work to be done.

People who immigrate to the United States often attempt to maintain their own cultural identity while understanding the new country they've come to, which creates a large region made up of a great number of cultures. Providing young students with multicultural media allows them to open their minds from an adolescent age while giving older students the same opportunity helps them battle biases and stereotypes they might hear from an outside source.

Some citizens who have become accustomed to a certain way of life believe immigrants should be forced to assimilate rather than integrate, but fortunately, multicultural literature helps to battle those negative and harmful ideals. Including literature that celebrates and recognizes all people, ways of life, ideas, and beliefs is essential to creating a well-rounded society.

Reviewing Books of Multicultural Literature

Fortunately, readers have a variety of choices when it comes to choosing a multicultural literature-based novel. In this section, three famous books will be reviewed and the elements, themes, and conflicts within them will be discussed. The books which will be reviewed are:

  • Esperanza Rising by Pam Munoz Ryan
  • The House on Mango Street by Sandra Cisneros
  • American Born Chinese by Gene Luen Yang

Common Themes

Most multicultural literature focuses on a set number of common themes, such as friendship, family, and love, but does so in a way that allows the reader to understand what their culture is like and how it is perhaps different from the reader who consumes it.

In Esperanza Rising, Pam Munoz Ryan creates a world of young adult historical fiction based on her experiences as a Mexican-American woman. The novel itself follows the main character Esperanza, the only child of affluent Mexican parents, and the events that follow the untimely death of her father. She discovers he was murdered and she must battle with this fact as she gets used to a new world without him. Wealth, privilege, and class are common themes within this novel because Esperanza is spoiled and entitled by her parents until her father dies. After his death, she deals with grief and loss and must grapple with her struggle to find hope and experience a rebirth. This novel is perfect for anyone who is dealing with a loss in the family and wants to learn more about Mexican culture.

In The House on Mango Street, readers have the ability to learn about Mexican culture as it exists in American society. Sandra Cisneros published the novel in 1984; it is structured around a series of vignettes (short chapters) that tell the story of a 12-year-old girl coincidentally also named Esperanza and what her life was like growing up in Chicago. Specifically, she lives in the Hispanic quarter of the windy city - a part of society that had yet to be unearthed on a country-wide level at the time of publication. Esperanza struggles with her identity as a Mexican-American girl and spends the majority of the book trying to find her place in the world. Some themes are women and femininity, gender, innocence, and the achievement of dreams. Since the story takes place as a series of short chapters, it is highly recommended for young readers who are just getting into multicultural literature.

Lastly, American Born Chinese by Gene Luen Yang is a different type of multicultural literature than what is usually seen: a graphic novel. This is most recommended for students who wish to learn more about other cultures but have issues reading more '"classic"' types of books. The story itself centers around a monkey king who becomes insulted by other rules; in order to prove he's better than they think, he begins practicing kung fu and increases his abilities until he is satisfied. Ultimately, he ends up showing off his skills and proving his worth. This work battles against Chinese insults and stereotypes prevalent within American society throughout the twentieth century. Some common themes are identity and prejudice, racism, and pride, stubbornness, and humility.

Conflicts in Multicultural Literature

Because multicultural literature battles against stereotypes and unfair prejudices, novels that focus on this theme have a multitude of conflicts. Most multicultural literature has a focus on internal and external conflicts, including the novels discussed in the last section. Mexican-American Esperanza battled internally to fight the stereotypes she had been riddled with her whole life, while the monkey king fought externally to show people he was better than they thought. Like most stories written with the intention of teaching the reader a lesson, multicultural literature can have a variety of issues the protagonist must face.

For example, in One Hundred Years of Solitude by Gabriel Garcia Marquez, the main conflict is human isolation. The isolated town of Macondo battles against the rest of the progressing world. They have a certain style of life, a more traditional type, while the rest of the world globalizes. This provides a direct link to the people who battle against society reading multicultural literature and experiencing cultural differences as a whole. The world is moving on and ever-changing and if people won't keep up, then they'll get left behind.

Benefit of Reading Multicultural Literature

A great number of experts believe all readers, regardless of age, gender, or race, can profit from experiences with multicultural literature. Some of the most important aspects and benefits of reading authors from a variety of backgrounds will be reviewed below.

  • It promotes unity and empathy. Students who read this type of literature report they feel less isolated and have a better understanding of the world as a whole.
  • It promotes friendship that spans across cultures. Students who learn about other cultures through reading are more likely to befriend someone who comes from a very different background from them or who speaks, acts, eats, or talks in a way they might not have understood before.
  • It encourages identity formation. By understanding that there are people across the globe who do not have the same experiences as them, students are able to accept who they are more easily.
  • It helps students look at and think more critically about the world as a whole, which helps society grow and become more accepting overall.

Lesson Summary

Multiculturalism is the existence of a variety of different cultures within one country. It shows that while no two cultures are the same, there is not one that is better or more important than the other. Allowing cultures to exist as separate units within the same society helps the nation grow and become more of a melting pot than it was even a few decades ago. The ease of international travel and the existence of social and electronic media has done what it can to show there is more than one way of life, but unfortunately, stereotypes still exist and the prejudices that come with them harm society as a whole. Multicultural literature helps to fill those gaps by allowing students and readers of all ages to experience a variety of new and different cultures from the author's eyes. Through an examination of themes, conflicts, and elements within multicultural literature, the reader becomes more open-minded and understanding of the experiences of others. It is important for multicultural literature to be integrated into society so the United States of America can grow into the country it was meant to be; one filled with understanding and acceptance, where anyone is capable of achieving the American Dream.

Definition of Multicultural Literature

Let us begin by first defining some key terms. Multicultural literature describes how people live in different parts of the world. In other words, we can learn about their culture and beliefs. It also presents an accurate representation of the culture it portrays and must be free from stereotypes, or beliefs about a particular group that are oversimplified or based upon generalizations. Through these accurate representations, we can build a clear understanding of the culture's heritage as it is portrayed in the text.

The Kite Runner

One novel that presents this well is The Kite Runner by Khaled Hosseini. Events such as kite running are deeply rooted in Afghan culture. In the story, set in Afghanistan and the United States, we follow the journey of our protagonist, Amir, as he struggles to gain the approval of his father, Baba. Told through a series of flashbacks mixed with events in the present, we accompany Amir on his quest for his approval and a way to make peace with the internal conflict he faces. Much of this conflict comes from the relationship with his father and his close friend, Hassan. Amir and Hassan are friends even though they are from different social classes, and throughout the novel, we see the cultural importance of friendship, family, and the division of classes in society.

Things Fall Apart

This type of relationship is also expressed in Things Fall Apart by Chinua Achebe, which explores the culture of the Igbo people of Nigeria. In his work, Achebe exposes us to the life of Okonkwo, leader of the fictional tribe of Umuofia. They are representative of the Igbo culture. Like Amir, Okonkwo experiences internal conflict that stems from his relationship to his father. He is ashamed of his father's laziness and lack of productivity.

As a result of this, Okonkwo struggles to build a reputation worthy of respect. However, Okonkwo sees much of his father in his son, Nwoye. Okonkwo sees Nwoye's lack of masculinity to blame for his interest in the Christian missionaries who have established themselves near to his village. Much of the novel focuses on the struggle between Okowno and his son and the culture clash between the Umuofia/Igbo people and the missionaries.

One Hundred Years of Solitude

Gabriel García Márquez craftily blends culture with fantasy in One Hundred Years of Solitude. The novel tells the story of José Arcadio Buendía and his wife, Ursula, as they leave their home to found the city of Macondo. Throughout the novel, Marquez takes the reader on a journey through time and frequently shifts between the past, present, and future. On our journey, we meet José and Ursula's descendants as we watch the citizens of Macondo struggle to hold onto traditions as the world changes around them.

Each new generation of their family represents the cultural and industrial changes of Macondo from a rural village to a modern town. In the town's final moments, their last descendant, Aureliano, manages to translate a mystical scroll that contains Macondo's history. Sadly, upon completing this task, a hurricane wipes out the town and all traces of its existence.

The Importance of Multicultural Literature

Gaining a better understanding of other cultures, such as those featured in the novels we've discussed, can help us gain insight into our own. We often accept things at face value because we have become accustomed to them. We sometimes avoid, intentionally or otherwise, questioning longstanding traditions and beliefs and may eventually forget their origin and meaning. It is important to remember the past, as it can help guide us in the decisions we make.

Lesson Summary

Multicultural literature can provide insight into a culture's thoughts and beliefs. As you read these stories, we can learn about the history, people, and experiences of other cultures. In turn, we can learn more about our own cultures and its values. We can also examine the truth, or fiction, behind certain stereotypes and formulate our own views.

Lesson at a Glance

Multicultural literature describes how people live in different parts of the world. In other words, we can learn about their culture and beliefs. Themes such as family, belonging, and preservation are common across all cultures. Three examples of multicultural literature are:

  • The Kite Runner by Khaled Hosseini: Afghanistan
  • Things Fall Apart by Chinua Achebe: Igbo people of Nigeria
  • One Hundred Years of Solitude by Gabriel García Márquez: Colombia

Learning Outcomes

Upon reviewing this lesson, you should be able to

  • Define multicultural literature
  • Describe some themes that are common in multicultural literature
  • Cite and summarize examples of multicultural literature

Frequently Asked Questions

What does being multicultural mean?

A multicultural person is someone who honors a variety of different cultures within their lifestyles. This can be someone who speaks multiple languages or has parents from two different nationalities. This could even be people who function successfully in more than one culture - such as having a multicultural mind, which means they process things in a way that considers multiple cultures.

Why is multicultural literature important?

Multicultural literature is important because it can be seen as an instructional device used to open the minds of students. It assists with understanding diversity in the classroom and the world as a whole. It helps students build respect for people of different cultures and lifestyles.

What does multicultural mean in simple words?

In simple terms, multiculturalism is the existence of many different cultures within one set group. For example, a large city that has different sectors dedicated to different lifestyles would be a good study of multiculturalism (i.e., Little Italy, China Town, etc.).
