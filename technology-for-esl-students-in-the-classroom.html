What is ESL Teaching?

English as a Second Language or ESL refers to students learning English in addition to their native or first language. The name itself is sometimes a misnomer, as students may be learning English as a third, fourth, or more language. Other term for these students include emergent bilinguals or English Language Learners (ELL). These students come from diverse cultures and backgrounds and have different proficiency levels in English. Furthermore, it is important to remember that ESL students come from a place of asset, not deficiency. They are acquiring the asset of an additional language, which is a complex process. ESL students require additional support through specialized instruction in order to develop their language skills in the classroom.

Teaching ESL focuses on developing speaking, listening, reading, and writing language skills. In an ESL classroom, teachers engage and support language development using various instructional strategies, such as visual aids, hands-on activities, and technology. Additionally, classrooms with ESL students should include culturally responsive materials and activities that reflect their diverse backgrounds and experiences.

One of the biggest struggles for ESL students in English classrooms is understanding the material taught for different subjects. Teachers can use various teaching technologies, including televisions, projectors and interactive whiteboards, tablets and computers, and multimedia materials like videos and recorders, to accommodate various learning styles and language abilities. Using technology in the classroom supports the learning experience for all students, particularly ESL students.

ESL Technology & Resources for the Classroom

Teachers can use technology to engage ESL students in language development.

Technology is a fundamental part of the classroom and plays a vital role in classrooms with ESL students. It influences the way teachers deliver instructions and the way students learn. Teachers now have a wealth of free or low-cost technical resources and tools to draw from that can aid instruction and argument learning.

Initially, ESL technology included basic tools like audio recording and playback devices such as tape recorders to capture spoken English. Cassette and VHS tapes have evolved into DVDs and digital media like videos and podcasts, providing access to English in a variety of formal and informal situations. Furthermore, interactive smart boards and tablets introduced language applications that allow students to practice basic language skills like grammar and vocabulary in fun and engaging ways. Finally, online platforms like YouTube and Google allow students to translate written and spoken English.

ESL technology resources help students develop language skills by providing them with relevant opportunities to practice language skills. Using technology like virtual meeting software, ESL students can interact with other English speakers from around the world, widening their exposure to the language. Platforms with leveled texts can provide ESL students with appropriately leveled reading material for many subjects in both fiction and nonfiction. At the same time, online writing tools can provide instant feedback on grammar, vocabulary, and tone. Lastly, many language-focused websites offer games backed by research in language development. These technology resources are well-known, accessible, easy to use, and do not require advanced computer skills.

Voice Recorders

Voice Recorders can be practical tools for ESL students and do not require advanced computer skills. Originally tape recorders served as voice recorders, but today most computers, tablets, and smartphones have voice recording apps preinstalled. While technology has evolved, the benefits of this tool remain the same.

  • Pronunciation Practice: Students can use voice recorders to practice pronunciation by recording speech, playing it back, and analyzing it. Additionally, they can use a voice recorder to listen to native speakers and compare pronunciation.
  • Conversation Practice: A voice recorder can record the class asking and answering questions individually or in pairs and then sharing the recordings with the teacher or classmates for feedback.
  • Presentation Rehearsal: Recording the delivery of a presentation and then listening to it can help students identify areas that need improvements, such as clarity, pace, and intonation.
  • Improve Listening Skills: Students can record lectures, podcasts, or other audio material to listen to and take notes in order to improve their listening comprehension and note-taking skills.

An example of how to use a voice recorder in the classroom is during a pronunciation lesson coupled with subject-specific content. The student could record themselves reading a passage from a textbook. Next, they would listen to the recording, noting mispronunciations and researching the correct pronunciation.

The most vital benefit of this technology is the self-assessment component. By listening to their spoken word, students can gain insight into their strengths and weaknesses and create learning goals that move their language development forward.

Video Animation & Editing

Using videos and animation in the classroom can greatly benefit ESL students. As an instructional tool, teachers can use short or long-form videos to improve the following language skills.

  • Listening Comprehension: Students can identify key information and understand the context of the conversations occurring in the video.
  • Speaking Skills: Students can record themselves summarizing something they have read, participating in a debate, or role-playing a situation that allows them to practice formal and informal speech and put these types of speech into context.
  • Vocabulary Development: Ask students to identify unknown words used in a video and use context clues to discover their meaning.

Additionally, students discussing videos they have watched allows ESL students to practice conversation skills with their peers. Since many ESL students are also adapting to a new culture, videos can help them explore different cultural aspects in a fun and relatable way.

Students also can benefit from creating and editing videos themselves. In the early days, they would have used a video camera, but today any smartphone or tablet has a camera and video recorder built in. Some of them even have basic editing software preinstalled. Students can benefit from writing the script, acting in the video, and analyzing their performances. Moreover, students could upload these projects to classroom websites or Google Classroom.

An example of implementing video technology in an English classroom with ESL students could include showing a video of a popular, historically important tourist attraction. Students could work in pairs to define new vocabulary and answer post-viewing questions. As an extension, two pairs could form a group and set out to shoot a video about a popular place to visit in their town.

Animated Slides

Animated slide presentations can hook students' attention and get them excited about developing their language skills. Some possible activities based on this technology include these options.

  • Animate a Story or Reading Passage: Students can work to animate the plot using leveled text, focusing on story elements and text structure.
  • Illustrating Vocabulary: Students can put their unique twist on vocabulary words, making them easier to recall.
  • Animating Figurative Language: Idioms and similes pose particular difficulties for second language learners. Using animated slides can help students build meaning through visual learning.

However, this technology does require more advanced computer skills and prior technology experience.

Interactive Activities

Interactive activities tap into social learning, which is learning alongside others through observing and imitating. This type of learning significantly increases langue acquisition. The relationship between observation, imitation, and feedback or reinforcement facilitates language acquisition, similar to how people first acquire language in infancy. Teachers can foster this connection by implementing a learning environment focused on interactive activities.

  • Texting: Normally prohibited in the classroom, teachers can build student enthusiasm using this technology in a real-life and meaningful way. Students benefit from practicing language skills.
  • Virtual Group Discussions: Using online discussion forums, ESL students can contribute to virtual class discussions, ask questions and practice expressing ideas and opinions in English.
  • Group Projects and Presentations: Peer-to-peer collaboration provides a natural and informal setting ESL students can feel comfortable practicing language skills.
  • Virtual Meetings and Webcams: With advances in virtual tools like virtual meetings and classroom software, ESL students can connect with other students from around the country and even the world.

ESL students often face isolation in the classroom. Using technology to enhance social learning offers them increased language acquisition and the opportunity to connect with peers and fellow language learners.

Virtual Walls & Posters

Technology can be used to connect ESL students with their peers and other language learners.

Although some ESL students may not have basic technology skills, teachers can still use technology in the classroom with virtual walls and virtual posters. An online platform allows teachers to build virtual walls and project them onto an ordinary wall. Students post definitions of vocabulary words, write sentences to share, or post replies to questions projected for the whole class to see and discuss. Teachers can also project images, and students can write short stories, list context clues to explore the situations, or even label using academic vocabulary.

Virtual posters are similar to blogs, only visual. Online platforms allow students to upload images, videos, and text to create a virtual poster are share it with the class. Students might construct virtual posters on selected topics. Like a traditional research report, students collaborate to research a topic and design a virtual poster reporting about it. A project like this encourages ESL students to practice their reading and writing skills. Finished products can be posted on classroom websites by teachers.

Benefits of ESL Technology in the Classroom

Technology has numerous benefits for the ESL classroom, including increased engagement, active participation, and improved learning skills. Interactive technology like online quizzes and educational games can supplement traditional classroom activities. Using these technologies benefits ESL students by allowing them to practice grammar, vocabulary, pronunciation, and comprehension in an interactive and, at times, social manner. Visual multimedia and interactive technology address multiple learning styles and make language acquisition easier.

Technology also plays a vital role in aiding teachers. Instant feedback provides help in identifying areas of need and driving the learning process. Teachers can further use technologies to provide feedback to students as well. Using technology, teachers can create dynamic and immersive learning environments that support ESL students. By hooking student interest, teachers provide language students with opportunities to participate in collaborative learning and practice language skills in more real-world situations.

Lesson Summary

English as a Second Language (ESL) or Emergent Bilinguals are students who are learning English in addition to a first language. These students come from diverse cultures, backgrounds, and levels of English acquisition. ESL students require specialized instruction to learn content along with a new language. One strategy is for teachers to use technology, which increases student engagement, language skills acquisition, and active participation. It also gets students excited about learning and does not require advanced computer skills. Teachers can find free or low-cost apps and programs to use in the classroom.

Tools such as voice recorders help ESL students with pronunciation, conversation practice, and listening skills since the students can record and play back what they said to make corrections as needed. Video and animation improve listening comprehension, speaking skills, and vocabulary development. Teachers can create virtual walls using online platforms and project the virtual wall on a screen or whiteboard. This allows students to write words for images shown, construct sentences from vocabulary words, or post responses to the teacher's questions that other students can view and the class can discuss. Virtual posters, similar to their paper counterparts, allow students to create virtual posters on any topic using images, videos, and text. Overall, many activities with multimedia resources facilitate social learning, where ESL students can observe and emulate English-fluent peers in a more natural language acquisition process.

Technology in the Classroom

No doubt most teachers are familiar with the saying, 'A picture is worth a thousand words.' Nowhere is this more evident than in the ESL classroom. How many times have we, as educators, seen the benefits of explaining a difficult concept or introducing new vocabulary with the help of visual aids? Well, technology allows for many ways in which to increase student understanding with more than just a picture! There are many tools to augment instruction so students can improve their speaking, listening, reading, and writing skills in a fun and engaging manner.

Likely, you are familiar with the classroom use of projectors, SmartBoards, Google, and YouTube. These tools augment instruction when presenting new information, explaining difficult concepts, or introducing vocabulary. Although these tools can make instruction more interesting for students (and you!), they are not the only ones available. In this lesson, we'll explore how technology can help ESL students improve their skill level on their own, as part of a peer group, or with you as a facilitator. So, let's look at some ideas for easy-to-implement technology that will engage and empower your students.

Voice Recorders

To improve their speaking and listening skills, ESL students benefit from hearing proper pronunciation as well as being able to hear their own. There are a variety of applications that allow you to record passages or vocabulary for your students. These applications allow students to then repeat and record what they hear as well as listen to what they said.

You could also utilize these recorders by providing students with a prompt. Students record themselves answering the prompt and then analyze their answers by playing the recording back or sharing the recording with you for feedback.

Some voice recording applications can be built into student laptops or tablet apps, such as GarageBand or VoiceThread. There are other applications that require no downloads. These online recorders allow users to record their voice, play back the recording, and give several options for sharing. Anyone with basic computer skills can use these.

Video & Animation

Creating and publishing videos can get ESL students excited about storytelling and sharing their work with an audience while at the same time building their oral communication skills. For instance, students could create videos where they summarize the highlights of an event, explain the steps in a process, or even animate a story of their own creation to be shared with the class. In addition, these videos could be uploaded to YouTube, Google Drive, or a classroom web page.

Video Editing Applications

Some programs allow users to upload images or videos to create professional looking videos. An easy-to-use video editing program is iMovie. WeVideo is another user-friendly, web-based platform that provides collaborative video editing capabilities. Typically, these applications require a bit of technical savvy, so they're for students who already have some technology skills. Nonetheless, they're fun tools to use!

Animated Slides

Another fun way for students to utilize technology in the ESL classroom is by animating a story. Whether based on a teacher-assigned story or one of their own, students can create animated slideshows using a variety of cloud-based platforms. One such platform is PowToon. Slightly advanced computer skills are required to create these types of presentations, so they're better suited for students with experience with other presentation software, such as PowerPoint or Prezi.

Interactive Activities

Projects involving student interaction and collaboration are another great way to utilize technology in the ESL classroom. Because group work requires student conversation, these situations develop English language skills and help students increase their English proficiency.

Virtual Walls

Tools for ESL students with limited technology skills include virtual walls, such as Padlet and other similar applications. They allow students to interact with each other and the teacher by posting responses that can be projected for everyone in the class to see. Students might be asked, for example, to construct an original sentence based on a vocabulary word. They can then add their sentences to the wall while their responses are projected for the class to see and discuss. Another possible use for virtual walls involves using images. Very easily, a teacher might post an image and ask students to write the word that the image represents. Student responses will be visible on the projected screen and can be discussed with the class.

Virtual Posters

Finally, teachers can also use virtual posters, which provide a way for students to collaborate while developing their language skills. Within these platforms, students can create multimedia presentations on electronic poster paper. One such platform is Glogster. It allows users to import sound clips, pictures, videos, and text. One great feature is the ability to create clickable links, which adds an interactive component to the presentation.

Virtual posters can be used in the ESL classroom in interesting ways. For instance, a teacher might task student groups with creating a poster around a specific topic. Students could then conduct research, and as they research and create their presentation, they also expand their reading and writing skills. The end result would be a visually appealing virtual poster that can be presented in class or posted onto a class web page. These projects are appropriate for ESL students with basic computer skills and online research experience.

Lesson Summary

The tools presented in this lesson represent some of the technology that ESL teachers can use to augment instruction. Some tools include voice recorders, video editing software such as iMovie and WeVideo, virtual walls such as Padlet, and virtual posters such as Glogster. Typically, these tools are free, easy to implement in the classroom, and do not require advanced computer skills.

Frequently Asked Questions

How can you use technology to teach ESL?

Teachers can use technology to teach ESL students as it helps facilitate the development of language skills. Using multimedia like videos and podcasts, video and voice recorders, and online sharing platforms can help ESL students practice their written, oral, and reading skills in English.

What is ESL?

ESL means English as a Second Language. It designates students who are Emergent Bilinguals or learning an additional language to their native language.
