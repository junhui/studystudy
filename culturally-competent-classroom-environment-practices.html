In this lesson, you will learn about strategies and behaviors that you can incorporate into your classroom to foster a more equitable learning environment for all of your students.

The Classroom Connection

Virtually all of us have had an experience when we felt misunderstood. Whether you have traveled abroad and experienced unfamiliar customs, attended an event full of strangers, or attended a sporting event supporting the opposing team, you have likely experienced the insecurity and anxiety associated with being the ''outsider''. The unfortunate reality is that many students experience these emotions every day they attend school. Today's classrooms are growing increasingly diverse, requiring teachers to consider many more perspectives and customs than they have in the past. However, there are relatively simple practices and behaviors that can help teachers establish a welcoming classroom culture for all of the students they serve.

What Is a Culturally Competent Classroom Environment?

A culturally competent classroom environment is an environment that not only acknowledges diversity among members of the school community, but also celebrates it. There is no ''silver bullet'' to fostering this type of classroom culture. However, it is very important that educators first evaluate their own behaviors before trying to instill these behaviors in their students.

Strategies for Creating a Culturally Competent Classroom

The strategies that follow incorporate self-reflective practices as well as student-centered activities.

Strategy #1: Start the day or class period off on the right foot.

Greet your students at your door every day. Welcome them to the classroom. Most importantly, greet them by name. Of course, this practice cannot typically start on the very first day of school. However, take the time to get to know your students' names (and correct pronunciations) as quickly as possible. Ask each student if he or she has a preferred nickname. A student's name is a big part of his or her identity. In greeting students by their names, you are acknowledging each of them as a valued individual in your classroom.

Strategy #2: Be aware of your own nonverbal communication.

Your students spend a great deal of time observing you, whether you are requesting their attention or not. It is human nature to ''wear'' our emotions like clothing. Be mindful of the message that you are sending to your students. Make eye contact with each of your students throughout the day. Avoid rolling your eyes, crossing your arms, or expressing frustration (verbally or nonverbally) when a student asks a question. Whether you intend to or not, these behaviors could marginalize some of your students, making them less likely to participate or ask questions.

Additionally, be mindful of where you position yourself in the classroom. Move around the room to ensure that your presence is felt by each of your students throughout the day.

Strategy #3: Divide your time equitably.

This practice is not a ''one size fits all'' approach. You always want to make sure that you are maximizing your instructional minutes. This means that you should deliberately divide your time among your students, based on individual student needs. Some students require more assistance than others. You will naturally be spending more time with your low expectancy, or lower performing, learners. However, if you are not careful, you could find yourself spending the majority of your time with a small group of students, thus limiting your interactions with the rest of them. Each of your students has different needs. Make sure that you are doing your best to meet the needs of all of your students.

Strategy #4: Put your students' work and cultures on display.

Bulletin boards are more than just classroom ''wallpaper''. Bulletin boards are an excellent way to celebrate the diversity in your classroom. Make sure that each of your students' heritages and cultures is represented somewhere in your room. When you have holiday celebrations, be mindful of the fact that not all students celebrate the same holidays that you do. It is important to celebrate all walks of life to show all of our students that our differences should not only be acknowledged, but celebrated.

Another characteristic of a culturally competent environment is the presence of student work on display. Celebrate the accomplishments of all of your students in some way, shape, or form. Show your students that you value their individual talents by showcasing them. You will never get tired of seeing the pride on their faces when they see their work hanging on display. This applies to children of all ages.

Strategy #5: Establish cooperative classroom practices that allow students to work together toward a common goal.

Cooperative learning is a classroom strategy that requires students to collectively perform an assigned task. Cooperative learning not only reinforces essential skills for future success, it also allows your students to interact with others who are different than they are. It is human nature to gravitate toward individuals whom we perceive as similar to us. Cooperative learning, when implemented effectively, is an excellent way to foster positive relationships among all of your students.

However, cooperative learning groups should be deliberately formed by the teacher. Allowing students to ''count off'' or choose their own groups offers little to no benefit to your classroom environment. Deliberately grouping students allows you to consider ability, emotional intelligence, culture, and many other factors. Whenever possible, consider forming heterogeneous groups. Heterogeneous groups are groups that are comprised of students with differing abilities and/or characteristics. These groups should change throughout the year to allow students the opportunity to work with a variety of individuals.

Lesson Summary

In order to establish a culturally competent classroom environment, you must first get to know your students. Learning students' names, using bulletin boards to put a variety of cultures on display, and showcasing student work around the classroom are great ways to recognize and celebrate your students as individuals. Establish classroom practices that allow you to maximize instructional minutes while ensuring that you are equitably dividing your attention among your students. Finally, form heterogenous groups to engage in cooperative learning activities throughout the school year. You are the role model in the classroom, and often times, students will emulate your behaviors. The more positive and inclusive you are, the more likely your students will follow suit.
