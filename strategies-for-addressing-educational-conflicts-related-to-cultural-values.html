Currently, our nation is experiencing great turmoil and conflict regarding cultural differences. Schools can lead the way to acceptance by teaching tolerance and leading discussions about healthy conflict resolution. Read more to learn about strategies that schools can use.

Addressing Conflicts

In August 2017, white nationalists took to the streets of Charlottesville, VA with torches to protest the removal of the Confederate statue of General Robert E. Lee. The very next day, those same protesters were met with anti-racist protesters.The result was a cultural conflict that left 3 dead, 19 injured and received several weeks of national attention. While this event was a community conflict, it was strongly related to the differences in cultural values.

The populations of schools across the nation are changing drastically. Schools are becoming more of a salad bowl. The salad bowl concept is the integration of cultures, mixed together like a salad, unlike the melting pot term which was once used. Diversity and cultural conflicts can be a complex topic especially in education. The question of who is right or who is wrong often comes into play. Teaching understanding and tolerance can help resolve the differences in opinions and conflicts and prevent disasters such as the one that occurred in Charlottesville. In education, the outcome truly depends on the dialogue and presentation of differences. This lesson will identify strategies for addressing conflicts related to differences in cultural values among students, teachers, parents/guardians, and/or the community.

Students

Addressing conflicts related to differences in cultural values among students can be easy. Oftentimes, students do not have deep-seated issues and they typically make friends with whomever they encounter based more on interests. Some specific strategies for addressing conflicts among students are:

  • Teaching Tolerance- Students can learn about tolerance in the context of regular lessons or as separate experiences. In regular lessons, students can learn about Social Justice and Equity and Equality in History related to literature in English. Separate experiences of teaching tolerance can come in the form of speakers or school-wide events that promote unity and peace such as 'Mix it up day' where students are encouraged to sit next to students of diverse backgrounds during lunch.
  • Peer Mediation-Students can learn how to resolve conflicts related to differences in cultural values from other students. Schools can identify certain students who can be trained in conflict resolution skills. When students are experiencing a conflict, namely related to cultural differences, they can meet with the peer mediators to mediate and talk through their problems. If this does not work, students can meet with a guidance counselor to help diffuse any negative situations before they become a major incident.

Teachers

Teachers typically come from a similar background, middle class and suburban. With over 80% of public school teachers being white, teaching diversity can be challenging. That doesn't mean that it is impossible though. Some specific strategies for addressing conflict among students and teachers can begin with supporting the previously listed student strategies. In addition, some teacher strategies include:

  • Professional Development-Teachers can read texts or take professional development courses around diversity. These courses can be in person at a conference such as the National Education Association or Teaching Tolerance or can be taken via webinar. Topics include: Leading with Diversity, Cultural Competency and Teaching Tolerance to students.
  • Lesson Plans-Teachers can integrate diversity in their everyday lessons. These lessons can be on resolving conflict and teaching students to value diversity. Some examples of integrating diversity in everyday lessons include teaching about stereotypes when teaching historical perspectives and teaching about poverty and awareness when studying units on class and economics.

Parents/Guardians and the Community

Keeping parents and community members in the loop about the school's diversity efforts will ensure their engagement and support. Some strategies to accomplish this include:

  • Newsletters and Communication-School leaders can send out monthly newsletters or information highlighting what the school is doing to address conflict over cultural values and differences. This communication can highlight student events such as 'Mix it up day' or teacher lessons on the study of poverty in economics class.
  • Public Acknowledgement-Schools and districts can spotlight their school demographics and the various opportunities to improve conflicts over cultural differences. This can be accomplished at board meetings or open community events held at the schools.

Lesson Summary

This year, our country has faced tragedies that have forced us to look at diversity and how to avoid conflict among cultural groups. Schools have become a salad bowl, an integration of cultures, that requires multiple methods to address conflict. These methods are through the students, teachers, and parent/guardians and community that can teach tolerance and address conflict.

  • Students can participate in activities such as 'Mix it up day,' where students sit next to someone different from them during lunch.
  • Teachers can integrate diversity in lessons to help teach tolerance and promote peaceful resolutions in conflict situations.
  • Parents/Guardians and the Community can attend public events sponsored by the school and district to spotlight what they are doing to improve conflicts over cultural differences.

In all cases, there are specific strategies that can help improve race relations and avoid cultural conflicts.
