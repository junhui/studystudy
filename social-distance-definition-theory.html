Social distance is the distance of the level of acceptance of other groups of people in a society. Learn about the definition and theory of social distance, and discover various characteristics that lead to social distance.

Social Distance

Two teenagers are talking about the upcoming school dance. One mentions a desire to ask a certain person to be his date. The other quickly says, 'Don't bother; she's out of your league!' What does that mean? Is it true that some people are 'out of reach' to others?

Social distance refers to the level of acceptance people have of others outside of their own social group or class. This level of acceptance is defined by their general feelings toward others, and the amount of social interaction they have with people whose characteristics are outside of their social norm. Social distance is a measure of perceived difference (or distance) among groups. It can be small, for example, when people are accepting of others, or large, when people reject other groups. As a social construct, social distance is a familiar issue. Many common phrases refer to social distance, such as 'out of your league' and 'birds of a feather flock together.' These social norms are ingrained in our collective consciousness.

Which social characteristics apply to social distance? How is it measured? Is there something we can do as a society to decrease social distance? Let's find out!

Social Characteristics

Some of the personal characteristics that lead to social distance include race, ethnicity, age, gender, and economic class.

Race and Ethnicity

Racial and ethnic discord is the most commonly referenced measure of social distance. Racial social distance is at its greatest in societies that have enslaved specific groups of people. Those enslaved are socially distant from those who are slave owners. Historically, we have seen the social distance between races in the United States decrease over time, as slavery and then segregation were abolished. Remember, though, that social distance refers to more than the divisions between people that are sanctioned by the government and that not everyone experiences it the same way. Some people in the U.S., for example, still exhibit extreme levels of racial social distance by not wanting to live or work with members of other ethnicities.

Age

The generation gap is the term given to the differences in perspective and opinions between generations. While some interaction and close social engagement can be seen among people of different generations, individuals typically engage in social relationships with people who are close to them in age.

Gender

Gender roles are taught through differentiated socialization, or treating boys and girls differently, beginning at the earliest age, which reinforces social distance between the sexes. Gender-related social distance could be one cause of the glass ceiling, or the lack of female representatives in top-level professional positions. Some cultures enforce the social distance between the genders through dress. For example, in strict Muslim cultures women are required to wear clothing that covers them almost completely in order to visually separate them from the men in their society.

Social Class

Social distance between economic classes happens when members of an economic class avoid or reject members of another class simply because of their economic standing. It can work both ways, with wealthy individuals choosing to isolate themselves from people in middle and lower classes, while those in lower economic classes avoid interacting with the 'posh'.

Measuring Social Distance

Social distance is a form of prejudice, which can lead to discrimination. In its simplest form, prejudice refers to having negative feelings about certain groups of people based solely on a categorical characteristic.

Social distance can be measured best through social observation or through scientifically conducted surveys. Surveys attempt to measure levels of social distance by quantifying the amount of aversion a person has to another group. For example, a survey question might ask a person to rate their feelings on a particular group from 1 to 10, with 10 meaning they would absolutely avoid another at all costs and 1 meaning they would seek out members of the group in question.

And while every society will experience some form of social distance, the type and level of social distance will vary depending on the culture of the group being measured.

Diversity in Interaction

Diversity in interaction is the key to decreasing social distance in any culture. The more often different groups interact with each other in everyday, positive ways, the more they'll relate to one another as equals. Social distance leads to the isolation of groups amongst themselves. When people avoid interacting with others, they begin to see those others as different, or even beneath, themselves. To overcome these negative perceptions and decrease social distance as a whole, people must be encouraged to diversify in all aspects of their lives. Familiarity with other cultures and groups can lead to understanding, which should mitigate the feelings of aversion and isolation.

Lesson Summary

Let's review. Social distance is a conceptual measure of the perceived distance people feel from differing groups. Categorical characteristics commonly associated with social distance include race, ethnicity, age, gender, and economic class. For example, the generation gap is a term given to the differences in perspective and opinions between generations. Gender roles are taught through differentiated socialization, or treating boys and girls differently, which reinforces social distance between the sexes and may be a cause of the glass ceiling. Social distance is measured through natural observation as well as scientific surveys in which people are asked to quantify their personal levels of avoidance of certain groups. Encouraging diversity in interaction is the key to decreasing social distance, as the more often different groups interact with each other in positive ways, the more they'll relate to one another as equals.
