Worksheet: Meeting Needs of Different English Learner Typologies in ELD Programs

Eduardo is a Generation 1.5 student. He makes mistakes in English that do not make sense given that Eduardo is fully fluent and is perfectly adapted to the American way of life. Eduardo's mistakes are probably due to:

  1. an influence of the home language

  2. hanging with friends who speak other languages

  3. family who does not speak

  4. a learning disability

All typologies of English learners should benefit from this approach as part of their language development:

  1. have a teacher who understands their home language

  2. attend bi-lingual classes

  3. have native speaker friends

  4. monitor their progress

Alejandra is a student who has had adequate schooling back home. The following is a great approach to help her develop her English skills:

  1. let Alejandra learn English by herself

  2. place Alejandra with English speakers only

  3. use Alejandra's previous knowledge

  4. get Alejandra to take bi-lingual classes

Filling gaps and having a progress plan are approaches that benefit the following typology:

  1. long-term English learner

  2. Generation 1.5

  3. newly arrived student with adequate schooling

  4. newly arrived with inadequate/interrupted schooling

English learner typologies have to do with this aspect of students:

  1. social skills

  2. home language

  3. ability to learn a second language

  4. educational background
