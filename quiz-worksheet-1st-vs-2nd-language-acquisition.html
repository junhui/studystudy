Worksheet: First vs. Second Language Acquisition | Overview & Comparison

https://study.com/first-language-vs-second-language-acquisition.html
The following is the most basic difference between first language acquisition (FLA) and second language acquisition (SLA):

  1. FLA is solely based in listening.

  2. FLA does not involve formal instruction.

  3. FLA is instinctive.

  4. FLA is based on universal grammar.

Timmy is a one-year old who has started trying to say short words. The following is NOT an aspect of Timmy's acquisition of English as his first language:

  1. Timmy consciously works to learn to repeat what he hears.

  2. Timmy's main learning resource is listening.

  3. Timmy might make mistakes when he first gets to speak.

  4. Timmy does not require instruction to develop the language.

Tania is a student from Bolivia currently living in the US. She is going to begin high school in the fall but is taking an English as a second language class during the summer. The following is NOT an aspect of her process to learn the language:

  1. Tania's English language learning is not part of her usual life.

  2. Tania's learning is based on previous grammar knowledge.

  3. Tania uses telegraphic speech at times.

  4. Tania requires instruction to learn the language.

In general, students who are acquiring English as a second language can make progress if this basic situation is in place:

  1. They memorize materials provided by the instructor.

  2. They have enough opportunity to interact with others.

  3. They speak only English at all times.

  4. They pay close attention to their instructor.

Luis and Steve are working together in a science class at an English-speaking school. Luis is originally from Costa Rica and he is a bit behind Steve because his English is limited. Despite this difference, Luis and Steve have all the following in common, EXCEPT:

  1. Both students might make mistakes when using scientific terms.

  2. Both students use context to learn science.

  3. Both students understand science better than they can explain it themselves.

  4. Both students need to make the same effort to learn science.
