Demographics for English Language Learners

During fall of 2020, about 5 million (or 10.3%) of the nation's public school students were English Language Learners, according to the National Center for Educational Statistics (NCES). How does this number break down by location or age? What is the most common home language? Read on for a more detailed look at ELL students in the U.S.

English Language Learners: Overview and Definition

English language learners (ELL) are students learning the English language while working towards the same academic achievements as their peers.

While many ELL students have immigrated to the U.S., most are American citizens; in fact, according to Education Week Magazine, in 2016, 82% of ELL students in pre-kindergarten to 5th grade (and 65% of ELL students from 6th grade to 12th grade) were born in the United States.

The most recent data from the National Center for Education Statistics (NCES) shows that the student population of English language learners is growing quickly, demonstrating an increase in value and need for ELL programs nationwide. Check out the tables below for an overview of the most recent NCES stats from fall of 2020.

ELL Students by State

English language learning students attend public schools in every state, though the distribution of students varies by region. States with the highest percentage of ELL students were typically located in the West and the District of Columbia. Those with the highest ELL student populations are outlined below:

State % of ELL Students
Texas 20.1%
California 17.7%
New Mexico 16%

The states with the lowest ELL student population are outlined below:

State % of ELL Students
New Hampshire 2.9%
Wyoming 2.7%
Montana 2.5%
Vermont 2.3%
West Virginia 0.7%

ELL Students by Locale

Currently, the highest ELL student populations are concentrated in more urban areas. Note that the percentages for each locale listed below are averages of more specific designations within each type. For example, the ELL student population in America's cities includes the percentage of students living in large urban areas (with a population of 250,000 or more) as well as small urban areas (population of less than 100,000).

Location Type % of ELL Students
City 13.7%
Suburban Area 10%
Town 7.2%
Rural Area 4.4%

Demographic Shifts

Though urban areas currently have the highest populations of ELL students, rural and suburban areas are experiencing rapid growth in this demographic. As such, the need for more adequate ELL support in public school systems (including teacher training and licensure) is also growing in these areas.

ELL Age Demographics

The highest percentage of ELL learners could be found in kindergarten through fifth grade. Generally, there is a population decrease that correlates with student age. One explanation is that many students attain proficiency in English as they approach the upper grades. The breakdown of ELL students by grade is as follows:

Grade % of ELL Students
Kindergarten 12.9%
First Grade 15.0%
Second Grade 14.7%
Third Grade 14.4%
Fourth Grade 13.3%
Fifth Grade 11.2%
Sixth Grade 9.9%
Seventh Grade 9.0%
Eighth Grade 8.1%
Ninth Grade 7.7%
Tenth Grade 7.4%
Eleventh Grade 6.4%
Twelfth Grade 5.6%

Note: 8.0% of ELL students were reported as Ungraded.

ELL Language Demographics

ELL students reported speaking a number of different languages at home. Here are the most common home languages for public school-enrolled ELL students nationwide:

Home Language % of ELL Students
Spanish/Castilian 75.5%
Arabic 2.6%
English 2.5%*
Chinese 1.9%
Vietnamese 1.5%
Portuguese 0.9%
Russian 0.7%
Haitian/Haitian Creole 0.6%
Hmong 0.6%
Urdu 0.5%

Note: *This may include students living in multilingual households or adopted students who live in an English-speaking home but grew up speaking a different language.
