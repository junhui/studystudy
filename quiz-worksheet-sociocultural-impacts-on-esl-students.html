Worksheet: Sociocultural Influences on ELL Students

https://study.com/sociocultural-influences-on-ell-students.html
An ELL in your class shows no enthusiasm at all. You confirm this when you ask the student about how s/he likes the class. Her/his answer is: 'I am here only because the school makes me learn English.' Your approach to this lack of motivation is:

  1. Praise students for their learning achievements.

  2. Try to convince her/him that English is necessary and useful.

  3. Ignore the unmotivated student.

  4. You suggest a school where students speak her/his native language only.

Why should you teach with material that includes cultural aspects of several English speaking countries or communities?

  1. So ELLs are not ignorant about the culture.

  2. So ELLs get ready to sign up for new courses such as history, geography, etc.

  3. So ELLs learn about the culture that is attached to the language.

  4. So ELLs learn new vocabulary related to the culture.

You teach both children and adults. Due to lack of time, you basically follow the same lesson plans for both classes with a few modifications. When teaching vocabulary to adults, you change this aspect of the lesson:

  1. Instead of teaching up to 15 words, a maximum of 12 new words is appropriate for adults.

  2. Instead of showing visuals for the new words, adults get a handout to read the words.

  3. Exclude pronunciation practice as adults are usually ashamed to speak.

  4. Exclude spelling exercises because adults do not need to learn to write.

The external motivation in an ELL class comes from you as a teacher. The following is the only aspect of the motivation you should not worry about:

  1. Harmonious environment among peers.

  2. The students' accent.

  3. The class physical environment, including: posters, decoration, etc.

  4. Interactive and interesting tasks for students.

If an ELL has a learning disability, your approach should be:

  1. Give easier tasks to the ELL to attend her/his learning disability.

  2. Let the special education team deal with the student.

  3. Give the ELL the exact same opportunity as other students have to practice and try exercises and assignments.

  4. Pay particular attention to the ELL so s/he feels as important as the other students.
