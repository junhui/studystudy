The following is NOT a reason why speaking/listening skills in California are interconnected when it comes to ELD and ELA:

  1. The state wants students to use English for social and academic purposes.

  2. The state has lots of long-term English learners.

  3. The state has frameworks that work across disciplines.

  4. The state wants to give students college and career readiness.

Caroline is an ELD teacher in California who has a class of emerging English learners. How can Caroline prepare lessons accordingly with this proficiency level?

  1. Caroline should read the ELA/ELD Framework.

  2. Caroline should ask grade teachers what students need.

  3. Caroline should consult the appropriate specific tasks in the CA ELD Standards.

  4. Caroline should focus on advanced vocabulary skills.

As per the California ELD Standards, the terms collaborative, interpretive, and productive refer to:

  1. typologies of English learners

  2. speaking/listening skills

  3. ELD proficiency levels

  4. aspects of tasks students should be able to complete

Lara is an ELD teacher in California. After her English learners finish reading an article, she asks students to discuss their opinions about the article. This speaking task focuses on being:

  1. collaborative

  2. productive

  3. interpretive

  4. interpretive and productive

While college and career readiness is a goal for all California students, ELD teachers should focus on taking speaking/listening skills to a college level in particular with this group of English learners:

  1. Generation 1.5

  2. under-schooled English learners

  3. students who interrupted their education

  4. long-term English learners
