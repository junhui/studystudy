As an English language teacher, Tara puts a lot of effort in making sure her students know how to write a great conclusion for an essay. The best Tara can do to improve this ability in students is:

  1. to give ELLs lots of examples of conclusions

  2. to get ELLs to write conclusions

  3. to give ELLs a list of components of a good conclusion

  4. to provide ELLs with an easy definition of conclusion

Andrew is a teacher of ELLs and wants them to become better readers. Thus, Andrew gives students a lot of randomly chosen articles, short stories, etc. to read, and then tests them on their general reading skills. Is this the right approach?

  1. Yes, because students learn to appreciate reading.

  2. Yes, because students will get inspiration.

  3. No, because the reading assignments must have a purpose that is connected to the assessment.

  4. No, because students need to read on subject content.

The following is an example of a formal assessment for an English language learner:

  1. reading aloud to the class while the teacher monitors

  2. reading to identify new vocabulary

  3. reading a passage in a standardized test

  4. reading exercise to answer comprehension questions

The following is a good command to present in a speaking test for ELL students:

  1. Speak about your home country.

  2. Tell me about your daily routine.

  3. Tell me all about you.

  4. Speak about holidays in the US.

Kate, an ELL teacher, has many years of experience working in different schools. She notices there is a gap in the academic performance of ELLs in subject content assessments as compared to native speakers. This is usual because:

  1. ELLs come from low educational backgrounds.

  2. ELLs are still in the process of learning English.

  3. ELLs come from poor countries.

  4. ELLs are not used to difficult assessments.
