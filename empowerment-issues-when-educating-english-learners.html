Empowerment Issues When Educating English Learners

When you have English Language Learners (ELLs) in your school, there are a few actions you need to take in order to ensure such students get the same schooling opportunity as others. This lesson discusses areas of empowerment for ELLs.

Why Empowerment?

Sandra is an English Language Learner (ELL) who recently moved to the US with her parents. This is the first time she attends an English-speaking school as she comes from Mexico. As a new immigrant, Sandra feels homesick often. She also misses her school back home where she had lots of friends and was comfortable speaking Spanish. Can you imagine being a child and having to adjust to a new country and school while learning a foreign language? Your ELLs may often feel overwhelmed and, for this reason, taking actions to include them in student empowerment is important. Student empowerment is the set of actions that school authorities and faculty perform so that students have a sense of being part of the school community where they choose to learn. Let's analyze them one by one.

Student Empowerment

Actions to promote student empowerment begin in the classroom and can reach as far as the students' parents. This lesson will provide some basic actions you can take that can give your ELLs a sense of belonging in their new community, as well as examples to illustrate how the action can be put in place.

Positive Environment

Some of Sandra's classmates began to laugh when they heard that she has an accent. Immediately after this occurrence, Sandra's teacher took action and spoke to the group of students who were laughing at her. The teacher not only made sure to clarify to students that laughing to others is cruel and inappropriate, but also asked those students to put themselves in Sandra's shoes. The school also has a zero bullying tolerance policy. Both the teacher's action and the school policy being reinforced on a daily basis ensure a positive learning environment for all students, including ELLs. When ELLs feel that the environment that surrounds them is positive, this naturally makes them feel good and, thus, they are in the right state to learn not only English but all curriculum content.

Keep in mind as well that a positive environment includes not only promoting respect among students, but also the physical school and classroom environment. A nicely presented school makes all students feel happy to be there. You can use attractive posters, positive messages on the wall, examples of proficient student work clearly displayed, etc.

Value of Cultural Diversity

Sandra's teacher assigns students a presentation on their favorite holiday. Sandra chooses to present on the parades people attend to then go to church on Corpus Christi, a Catholic holiday. While Sandra's classmates are totally unfamiliar with Corpus Christi celebrations, her teacher sees in this a great opportunity for all students to learn and value other cultures. Thus, she encourages Sandra to bring pictures of the celebrations and to explain in detail how Mexicans celebrate this holiday. Sandra can even include some Spanish vocabulary that has no translation into English because it is about specific outfits people wear or customs. When teachers encourage ELLs to teach their culture and even language to others in the school, they promote the value of cultural diversity. The more your students learn about other cultures, the more tolerant and understanding they can become.

Active Communication

When Sandra's parents attend a parent evening, the school makes sure to have a Spanish interpreter in place in case this is needed. This way, the school ensures clear communication between parents of ELLs and their teachers. When schools ensure active communication between ELLs' parents and teachers, they empower students because well-informed parents can make better decisions for their children.

Inclusion of ELL Families

Sandra's school organizes lots of family activities throughout the year. Every time there is an event, the school makes sure to give ELLs a letter to take home inviting their parents to take part in the activity. Due to language barriers, the school sends the letters to ELL families in English as well as their home language whenever possible. This example illustrates that schools can and should include all ELLs families in school events regardless of language barriers. When parents attend events, they can always meet other parents who speak their language or parents with whom they can practice their English skills. This is basically about including ELL families in the normal activities all members of the school community can expect to be part of.

Respect for Decisions

While Sandra's parents agree and are aware of the need their daughter has to be in an ELL program, this is not the case for every other ELL parent. For instance, Sandra has a classmate who comes from Italy. His parents decided to opt out of the ELL program the school offers so that he can develop English language skills. The school accepts and respects this decision. However, the school is careful to document the parents' decision. In addition, the school clarifies to parents that opting out of tests that involve English skills is not possible as per federal law. When parents are allowed to make specific decisions that might affect their children's academic progress, schools are called to respect those decisions. However, the example illustrates the need for your school to document parents' decisions and to inform them of all of the consequences of their decision.

Lesson Summary

Student empowerment is the set of actions that school authorities and faculty perform so that students have a sense of being part of the school community where they choose to learn. Student empowerment should include ELLs to help them overcome the struggle it is to adjust to an English-speaking school while learning the language. There are many actions you can take to exercise student empowerment when it comes to ELLs. The actions are:

  • Create a positive school and classroom environment.
  • Give value to cultural diversity by encouraging ELLs to show their culture.
  • Ensure active communication between ELL parents and school.
  • Include ELL families in all school activities.
  • Respect the decisions ELL parents make regarding their children's education.
