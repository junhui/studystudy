How can instructors use their knowledge of negative language transfer to benefit their students?

  1. Instructors can convey only knowledge that doesn't include negative transfer so that their students can become proficient in certain aspects of the new language.

  2. Instructors can select appropriate approaches, materials, and strategies if they know what negative transfers to expect.

  3. Negative transfer, unfortunately, cannot be used to any benefit.

  4. Negative transfer slowly decreases, so instructors don't need to worry about it.

What is negative language transfer?

  1. When borrowing a word from L1 results in a correct use of that word in L2.

  2. When L1 and L2 follow the same syntax rules.

  3. When the direct translation of a sentence from L1 results in a grammatically distorted sentence in L2.

  4. When the direct translation of a sentence from L1 results in a grammatically correct sentence in L2.

What is language transfer?

  1. Applying rules and norms from L1 in writing in L1.

  2. Applying rules and norms from L1 to L2.

  3. Using words that exist in the same or similar form in L1 and L2.

  4. Avoiding words that exist in L1 and L2 in the same or similar form.

What is an example of positive language transfer?

  1. When we translate a sentence word-for-word and it doesn't come out correctly in L2.

  2. When we misspell words in L2 because they exist in our L1 but they are spelled differently.

  3. When we use our L1 knowledge to translate an idiom and it comes out correctly in L2.

  4. When we mispronounce sounds because they don't exist in our L1.

In which instance is negative language transfer likely to occur?

  1. In Spanish, the word is 'correcto' and in English, it's 'correct'.

  2. In German, people use 'Batterie', and in English, people use the word 'battery'.

  3. In Korean, there is no 'v' sound, and in English, many words start with a 'v'.

  4. In Chinese, the sentence structure follows the subject-verb-object model, and in English, sentences follow the same subject-verb-object model.
