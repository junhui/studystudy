You have a student with limited English proficiency in your math class. Which of the following is an example of a performance-based assessment this student could use to demonstrate understanding?

  1. Asking students to look at pictures of math patterns in textbooks

  2. Giving the student a list of mathematical word problems to solve

  3. Having students work in small groups to create a pattern and relevant acronyms

  4. Having students work in small groups to answer an essay question pertaining to math

What is one difference between traditional assessments and performance-based assessments?

  1. Traditional assessments are typically much easier for English language learners.

  2. Traditional assessments ask students questions about how to do something, while performance-based assessments ask students to complete the task being learned.

  3. Traditional assessments are a more accurate and reliable measure of student knowledge because they are standardized.

  4. Traditional assessments generally take much less time to complete than performance-based assessments.

Which one of the following is an example of a performance-based assessment?

  1. Reading a chapter from a text book

  2. A multiple-choice test

  3. A fill-in-the-blank worksheet

  4. Writing a letter to a friend

Students are learning about the Great Depression in social studies class. Which one of the following would be an example of a performance-based assessment an English language learner student could use to demonstrate understanding of this time period?

  1. Reading a textbook chapter that relates to the Great Depression aloud

  2. Working with a partner to read an article about the Great Depression

  3. Completing a fill-in-the-blank worksheet about the Great Depression with a partner

  4. Interviewing a grandparent or other adult about his or her experience during this time, and reporting findings to the class

Which of the following is NOT an example of a performance-based assessment?

  1. Students write a journal entry from the perspective of a historical figure.

  2. Students retell a story by placing a series of pictures in chronological order.

  3. Students complete a worksheet to match important dates with their historical events.

  4. Students interview each other about a challenging experience in their own lives.
