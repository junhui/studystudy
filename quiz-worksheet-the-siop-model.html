Why is interaction an important component within the SIOP model?

  1. It gives the teacher a break from being the main voice in the classroom.

  2. It allows students to learn from one another as they practice the skills being taught in the classroom.

  3. It provides opportunity for students to work on worksheets together.

  4. It shows the teacher which students are pulling their weight in the classroom.

What is the SIOP model?

  1. SIOP is the Student Instructed and Observed Performance method of instruction.

  2. SIOP is the Sheltered Intervention Observation and Performance model, a research-based method of instruction.

  3. SIOP is the Sheltered Instruction Observation Protocol, a research-based method of instruction.

  4. SIOP is the Student Interest Observation Protocol, a data-based method of instruction.

How does the SIOP model work?

  1. SIOP instructional components are combined with teaching strategies in order to meet the content and language needs of ELL students.

  2. Students are assessed, then SIOP instructional strategies are presented to each group according to their comprehension level.

  3. SIOP components are used, as ELL students are instructed individually, in order to ensure their content and language needs are met.

  4. The SIOP components are presented one at a time until each student is able to understand and show complete comprehension of both content and language objectives.

Which of the following is NOT involved when it comes to the process of lesson planning according to the SIOP model?

  1. Utilize multiple methods of content delivery (audio, visual, charts, etc.).

  2. Complete your overall assessment of the lesson.

  3. Clearly define content objectives, based on the previous question, in student friendly language.

  4. Include themes, standards, topic, materials, and vocabulary.

Which of the following should NOT be done when the SIOP process is completed?

  1. Rather than assess it right away, plan to review it quickly before using it again next time.

  2. Get feedback from students regarding what they learned and what they still do not understand.

  3. Review content and language objectives with the class, to see if students believe objectives were met.

  4. Complete an overall assessment at the end of the lesson to see what modifications may need to be made.
