ELD & ELA Standards in Oral & Media Communications in California

Students in California (who are not deaf or hard of hearing) have to be able to use language to effectively communicate in an oral manner and through media. This lesson explores what strategies teachers use to prepare their students to meet this California challenge.

ELA/ELD Framework

Let's meet Stewart, a California teacher of English language learners (ELLs). Stewart tells us that his work consists of helping his students with English Language Development (ELD), which is the set of skills that allows students to become proficient in English. In addition, Stewart plans his lessons so that his ELLs also keep up with aspects of English Language Arts (ELA), which involves knowledge of subject content. For instance, Stewart helps his ELLs with vocabulary for math. The reason why Stewart works like this is that, in California, all teachers adhere to the ELA/ELD Framework, which is for public schools. As part of this framework, there are many aspects of the use of language.

In this lesson, we focus on the analysis and evaluation of oral and media communications and assessing the appropriateness of different media as instructional tools for English learners.

Language for Meaningful Interaction

Stewart tells us that California students are successful in school and also in their future careers because they learn to use language in a meaningful way. Are you wondering what this means? Specifically, the ELD Standards state that interacting in meaningful ways is an important part of the collaborative skills people should have as they listen and talk in a conversation.

Let's illustrate. Imagine a student who is fluent in English but produces tangled sentences when writing an email. Or, imagine a student who, instead of answering a question someone asks, begins to talk about their opinions and ignores the question. To avoid this, California teachers work with students so they learn to communicate effectively in an oral manner and through a variety of media, such as email and blogs.

Let's look at how students use this knowledge, aside from the benefit for their personal growth.

Listening and Speaking in Tests

In California, ELLs take not only the usual academic standardized tests all students take, but in addition, ELLs must demonstrate their progress in the English language through a state-adopted test. Both types of tests have a speaking and listening portion.

Now, we ask Stewart to tell us about the strategies teachers apply to prepare their students for such tests. In short, how do teachers prepare students so they use language in meaningful ways? Let's see.

Practice of Oral Responses to Questions

Stewart tells us that a first strategy is to practice question/answer exercises with students. For instance, Stewart gives his ELLs an assignment to interview a peer about their usual weekends. However, the interview exercise goes back and forth because the interviewee can also take turns to ask questions. As students do this exercise in an oral manner, Stewart monitors them by walking around the class. Stewart makes sure students get used to responding to questions, not only with appropriate information, but with appropriate words to the topic. This practice, done frequently with a variety of topics, helps students build the habit of using language to effectively communicate in conversation.

Practice with Diverse Situations

Stewart wants his students to be able to respond to many different situations with the appropriate language. To achieve this, Stewart often poses imaginary situations to his ELLs, using a prompt such as, 'What would you say to...'. To illustrate, he may ask students, 'What would you say to your mother if she tells you not to go out to the park because it is too cold?' Students know the idea is to persuade their mother and come up with persuasive responses such as, 'I promise to dress really warmly', or 'I can use a bit of cold air because it is too hot in here.' This way, students develop the ability to appropriately respond in a variety of situations.

Use of a Variety of Media

Stewart is aware that part of the ELA/ELD Framework expects students to communicate ideas through a variety of media. Stewart knows this is important because of the predominance of technology nowadays. For this reason, Stewart gives his students the chance to communicate orally and in a written manner through a variety of available media sources.

For instance, last week, Stewart's ELLs made a video in which they tell others how to prepare recipes from their home countries. This week, Stewart's ELLs are writing a blog with the title 'Why I love learning English'. Stewart monitors all these activities to guide his students regarding the wording they use, as well as to reinforce English language conventions. To sum up, California students are able to apply their knowledge of the English language to current media tools of communication.

Assessing the Appropriateness of Varied Media

Stewart uses a variety of media to teach his students, including Internet magazines, newspapers, blogs, video tools, email, etc. He assigns his students online readings so that they can then discuss or write about their views. However, Stewart first assesses the appropriateness of such sources through two aspects. First, he considers the language level of his ELLs and second, the grade-level.

For instance, there is a very interesting article on volcanoes, a topic his ELLs in fifth grade are currently studying in their Earth Science class. However, the vocabulary in the article is beyond the students' level. Thus, Stewart finds an easier text. In addition, there is certain media that the ELA/ELD Framework suggests for students based on grade level. For example, ELLs in Kindergarten are not expected to make a video to communicate.

Lesson Summary

In California, English language learners (ELLs) attend not only English Language Development (ELD) sessions, but also English Language Arts (ELA) classes. Teachers assist students by following the ELA/ELD Framework for public schools. This framework includes the use of language for meaningful communication as a goal, which not only ensures students' personal success, but also their academic success in the speaking/listening portions of standardized tests both for ELD and ELA. The strategies teachers apply to help their students develop these skills include practicing with question/answer exercises, appropriate responses in varied situations, using a variety of media to communicate, and identification of grade-level appropriate sources.
