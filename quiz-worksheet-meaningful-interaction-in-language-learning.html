A teacher gives her students the chance to chat once a week freely about any topic they want. While students talk, the teacher catches students saying s/he don't. The following is what the teacher can say afterwards:

  1. 'I heard you use incorrect subject-verb agreement during this week's chat.'

  2. The teacher can review the correct grammar rules during class without referring to any students or the specific weekly chat.

  3. The teacher should not say anything because grammar is not important

  4. 'I heard you making this grammar mistake.' (while pointing at the students who made the error)

The main point of applying meaningful interaction strategies in a language learning classroom is:

  1. to get students talking even if their grammar is incorrect

  2. to get students developing their communicative competence

  3. to get students practicing pronunciation

  4. to get students to recognize their grammar errors

Mr. Johnson takes his English language learners to the zoo. While in the zoo, Mr. Johnson should encourage students to do the following for meaningful interaction:

  1. write down whatever they want about the zoo

  2. interview other people who are visiting the zoo

  3. read the museum's brochure

  4. talk with peers and zoo employees

The following activity in an English language learning class illustrates the application of the theory of meaningful interaction:

  1. students talk to a member of the military who is invited to the classroom

  2. students orally give the answers to a reading comprehension exercise

  3. students give answers to a list of questions about news they read

  4. students form complete sentences based on a list of verbs they're given

The following is an example of meaningful interaction that uses the student-to-material strategy:

  1. students work in pairs to identify adjectives in the text

  2. students give an oral summary of what they read, using the vocabulary in the text

  3. students write a summary of what they read, using the vocabulary in the text

  4. students provide information on what they read in their own words
