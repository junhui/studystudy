What is ELD in Education?

ELD (English Language Development) is a teaching strategy used in schools that focuses on students who are not native English speaking and are explicitly learning to read, write, listen, and speak in academic English. This strategy emphasizes the development of fluency in the English language. The purpose is to develop English proficiency, with learning content knowledge as a secondary focus. In the classroom, the primary goal of ELD is to learn the language, and instruction is based on the student's level of English proficiency. ELD school lessons are taught in English only and students are expected to only communicate in English, not their native languages.

There are two types of ELD in education instruction in the classroom: integrated and designated. Both types have the goal of developing English proficiency, but they differ in their approach during the school day.

  • Integrated ELD: supports language needs during regular lessons; students remain in the regular classroom and are able to learn grade-level content; objectives come from subject lesson standards
  • Designated ELD: supports language needs during separate instructional time; the focus is on developing language skills; lessons planned to help learners develop proficiency in the English language; focus on English proficiency to aid students in understanding grade-level content

ELD Instructional Models

There are many ways to meet students' needs when designing English Language Development (ELD) instruction. ELD education can be provided to ELD students in different ways. The main models for delivering English Language Development in the regular classroom are outlined below.

  • Blended Services: Students are grouped by language proficiency, across different classrooms and/or grade levels, in small groups of explicit ELD instruction taught by teachers, specialists, and/or aides. ELD students are grouped by their level of English mastery and grouped with peers on the same level.
  • Direct Instruction: Whole group lessons are followed by differentiated assignments based on language proficiency. Small groups are pulled to target individual needs. ELD students are included in the same whole group lesson with the whole class, then in small ELD English proficiency groups.
  • Centers: Students are grouped by language proficiency and rotate to differentiated activities in centers. The teacher leads a group, while others are involved in independent activities. ELD students are able to move from center to center with peers who have the same English proficiency.
  • Cooperative Groups: Cooperative activities are assigned by language proficiency. The activities promote listening and speaking and can be used in conjunction with other models. ELD students work together in groups with their peers of the same English proficiency.

What is SDAIE?

SDAIE (Specially Designed Academic Instruction in English) focuses on the idea of students learning content knowledge as a primary focus. The SDAIE model's purpose is for students to learn English through their content learning. In this model, grade-level material is taught in subject areas such as language arts, mathematics, science, and social studies, and students learn the English language through the content. SDAIE is sometimes called Sheltered Instruction. Sheltered Instruction implies that instruction may look different for English language learners than for students whose primary language is English.

The focus of ELL SDAIE strategies is academic language in the content areas. Students learning with this model must be prepared with English vocabulary and definitions to help with understanding throughout the lesson. There are practices that teachers can incorporate to help students bridge the gap and make the most out of SDAIE instruction. These practices include activating prior knowledge and using visual aids, manipulatives, and graphic organizers. Teachers can also provide hands-on learning experiences, use simplified language, and continually monitor progress to meet students' needs.

Features of SDAIE: List of SDAIE Strategies

SDAIE has many aspects that teachers can incorporate to support student learning through the academic instruction content in the classroom. Some of these features are included in the following SDAI strategies list:

  • Bridging or Activating Knowledge: Teachers can use pre-learning activities such as brainstorming to make connections and provide students with support. Other strategies include providing images, text previews, and vocabulary to prepare students for learning by building and activating prior knowledge.
  • Contextualization: Demonstrations, videos, hands-on learning experiences, and modeling give students experiences that they can relate to and build on unfamiliar concepts. These experiences aid students in developing concepts that they may not have any experience to build upon.
  • Modeling: Teachers model the language they want students to use, or use peer interactions, small groups, or group discussions to give students opportunities to listen and speak in English to build their skills. The more practice students have and the more English they hear, the more their skills will develop.
  • Representations: Students use drawing and/or presentations to demonstrate their understanding of concepts and language skills through the content.

Commonalities of ELD and SDAIE

The ELD and SDAIE education models have different approaches, but their main goals are the same. They both aim to meet students' needs and develop their English proficiency. ELD and SDAIE in education are both designed to meet students' needs in a nurturing and low-stress environment. Similar strategies are used, including using prior knowledge to link new information, progress monitoring, visual aids, and finding connections.

Lesson Summary

ELD (English Language Development) focuses on students learning to read, write, and speak in English by focusing on language skills. Students are only taught in English and instruction is designed according to the student's language proficiency. Instruction in the ELD model can either be integrated or designated. Integrated ELD instruction occurs during regular instructional time, whereas designated ELD instruction occurs during a separate time. There are also different models of ELD instruction, including blended services, direct instruction, centers, and cooperative groups. These models give teachers and other instructional staff different classroom structures to meet students' needs. SDAIE (Specially Designed Academic Instruction in English) focuses on students learning grade-level content as a primary focus. Students in the SDAIE model learn English through content instruction. Through the SDAIE model, teachers use various methods to support student learning, such as modeling and activating prior knowledge. SDAIE is sometimes also referred to as Sheltered Instruction.

Although the two instructional models have different approaches, their goals of meeting students' needs and developing English proficiency are the same. Both methods aim to meet students' needs in a nurturing and low-stress environment. Both methods use strategies such as using prior knowledge to link new information, progress monitoring, visual aids, and finding connections as ways to strengthen English language development in the K-12 classroom. When these two models are used effectively in the classroom, students' growth in English proficiency will be evident.

Learning English

As an English language learner in middle school, Shin Lee is finding herself faced with choosing between two different programs intended to help her with academics and in the development of her use of the English language. The two programs available at her school include ELD and SDAIE. Both programs have their merits and limitations. Shin Lee wants to participate in the program that is best suited for her individual needs. Let's explore her options.

ELD

The acronym ELD stands for English language development. ELD is an instructional model designed to help English language learners develop fluency in their use of the English language.

Features

ELD focusses on the development of English in every academic domain. This includes reading, writing, listening, and speaking, as well as other language processes like phonology, morphology, syntax, and basic language structure and function. Academic instruction is based on the student's current levels of English acquisition and can be differentiated to meet the needs of beginning, intermediate, and advanced level language learners.

ELD can be taught as a separate block of instruction in and of itself, or it can be included within regular classroom instruction, providing that the teacher is mindful of the different needs and levels of development of the English language learners in the classroom.

Benefits

One of the benefits of ELD is its focus on the development of English as a primary goal.

Limitations

Because its focus centers on the development of English language skills, academic development often becomes a secondary goal.

ELD focuses on developing fluency in use of the English language.

SDAIE

The acronym SDAIE stands for specially designed academic instruction in English. SDAIE focuses on teaching English as part of academic content (such as in Social Studies or Science classes). In most cases, instruction is designed so that students are able to receive support in their primary language as they access English in their academics.

Features

SDAIE is more about strategies used within regular instruction and is intended to help English language learners learn academic content while developing English language skills. Another name sometimes associated with SDAIE is sheltered instruction. This simply means that instruction looks different for English language learners than it might for regular education students.

SDAIE is often associated more with older elementary students into the middle and high school grades because of its tendency to be more focused on academic language than on the basic use of English.

Benefits

SDAIE focuses primarily on academics as it seeks to support English language learners throughout their education. Learning English within the academic backdrop is the main focus.

Limitations

The absence of an emphasis on the basic use of English may slow down the developmental process of learning English as a second language.

SDAIE develops English as part of academic language.

Comparison

Differences

Goal and Purpose

  • ELD: Purpose is to develop proficiency and fluency in English. Content knowledge is a secondary goal.
  • SDAIE: Purpose is to develop academic knowledge in the content areas. English proficiency is a secondary goal.

Focus

  • ELD: Follows a sequential process with instruction centering on current levels of proficiency in English.
  • SDAIE: Follows grade-level content and processes.

Instruction

  • ELD: English only.
  • SDAIE: English is primary but the first language is used, as needed, for clarification and support.

Commonalities

Goal and Purpose

Both models share the goal of strengthening English language development for students in grades K-12.

Focus

ELD and SDAIE both aim to create an atmosphere of instruction that is low-stress and focused on nurturing learners as they develop skills in English. There is also a common emphasis on the acceptance of and respect for diverse learners and, therefore, diverse cultures.

Instruction

Both approaches promote linking new information to prior knowledge in order to make important connections. There is a common emphasis on applying the learning to real-world examples and experiences. Both models also emphasize the use of visuals and other aides to support and strengthen learning, and try to reach out to all learning modalities. Frequent checks for understanding are also common in both models.

Lesson Summary

As Shin Lee seeks to decide which program is best for her, she will want to remember that:

ELD stands for English language development and is a program that puts emphasis on the actual development of the language with academic instruction coming in as a secondary focus. SDAIE stands for specially designed academic instruction in English and places its focus on the development of academic knowledge with the emphasis on English language instruction coming in second. There are specific commonalities and differences in their approaches, but in the end, both programs seek to provide support in strengthening English language development for English language learners in grades K-12.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the purpose of ELD?

ELD instruction aims to develop English proficiency in reading, writing, speaking, and listening. Therefore, content knowledge is a secondary goal in the ELD model.

What are the components of SDAIE?

The components or strategies used with the SDAIE model are bridging or activating knowledge, contextualization, modeling, and using representations. These components help students meet the goals of learning content standards.

What is the difference between SDAIE and ELD?

The difference between the SDAIE and ELD models is the instructional focus. The SDAIE model focuses on content knowledge, and English language proficiency is secondary. The ELD model focuses on English language development, and content knowledge is secondary.
