Adalie, an English learner from France, reads the combination 'ou' as 'oh' and 'eeooh', respectively. When the teacher asks Adalie if 'ou' is present in French, Adalie tells the teacher the sound in French is 'u'. Luckily, it is the same in English. Now, the teacher can:

  1. Get the student to pronounce as in French at all times to then correct her.

  2. Give the student auditory discrimination activities.

  3. Ask Adalie to forget about French and focus on English.

  4. Use French as a resource when appropriate.

Contrasting the sound of words like 'cat/cut' for an English learner is particularly helpful if the student is:

  1. in kindergarten

  2. an adult learner

  3. a teenager

  4. a Spanish speaker

An English learner keeps saying things like 'chower, chining' instead of 'shower, shining'. The following is an example of explicit instruction to develop phonemic awareness in this English learner:

  1. Teacher tells the student 'shower' sounds the same as 'shoe' or 'shorts'.

  2. Teacher tells the student that he needs to improve the pronunciation of 'sh'.

  3. Teacher pronounces 'shower' correctly right after the student mispronounces.

  4. The teacher gives the student a list of words with the 'sh' sound to read aloud.

An English teacher finds a short and fun text with a lot of words that include the consonant cluster 'th' at the beginning of words. This is great because students are struggling with this. The following is the best practice to promote phonemic development:

  1. The teacher asks students to read the text aloud.

  2. The teacher reads aloud and students follow the text.

  3. The teacher reads aloud to students so they get to listen.

  4. The teacher asks students to find words with 'th' in the text.
