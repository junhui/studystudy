In this lesson, you can explore the framework that guides the California initiative to have English Language Development (ELD) along with English Language Arts (ELA) standards in classrooms across the state.

Merging ELA and ELD

Let's meet Cristina, a Mexican student new to the United States. If Cristina were learning English as a second language in some states, she could be at a school where an English language learning teacher focuses on her need to learn English. Meanwhile, the school curriculum would be rich in other subjects like history or science. Cristina would miss much of the instruction of those subjects because her teachers' focus would be on helping her develop her English skills first.

This is precisely what does not happen in California because in this state, there is an interesting framework. The framework stipulates that English Language Development (ELD) standards support the English Language Arts (ELA) standards. In short, these two standards are interrelated because California has a diverse student population that demands the need to merge both ELA and ELD. Perhaps luckily for Cristina and her education, she ends up in California.

California has a diverse student population that needs to learn not only core subjects like language arts and history, but also needs to learn the English language.

ELA and ELD Definitions

Let's define ELD and ELA. English Language Development (ELD) standards are the set of skills a student should have to express ideas efficiently and confidently in English (ability to read, write, speak). These are referred to in California schools as CA ELD Standards. English Language Arts (ELA) standards are the set of literacy skills in subjects such as history/social studies, science, and technical subjects. These are referred to as CA CCSS for ELA/literacy (CCSS means Common Core State Standards). Let's discuss now how ELA and ELD relate.

The principle behind the idea of merging ELA and ELD is that all these diverse students deserve to receive a world-class education that involves quality communication in English along with knowledge of subjects like science, history, etc. Through Universal Access to the Language Arts Curriculum, California prepares its diverse student population for college and a bright future career. Let's discuss universal access now.

Universal Access to the Language Arts Curriculum

As we know, a teacher in California could have a classroom with students who have very diverse needs and backgrounds. In addition, the state expectations are that teachers provide all students with high-quality access to curriculum and instruction. The goal is to meet or exceed the state's standards for language arts and language development. Now, you might wonder how a teacher can do this.

Luckily, the California Department of Education provides teachers with a document called Reading/Language Arts Framework for California Public Schools. This is a guiding document that provides teachers with a blueprint so they know how to organize their instruction to meet the standards the state expects. Here, we concentrate on Chapter 7 of this framework, which discusses Universal Access to the Language Arts Curriculum.

As per Chapter 7, ELA standards support ELD standards. Here, you have ways in which this support happens:

  • Assessment of students to determine their proficiency in English. For example, Cristina takes an English language placement test that allows her teachers to determine if she is a beginner, an intermediate, or an advanced English learner. In turn, this helps her teachers design lessons that meet her individual needs.
  • Immersion from the beginning in an effective English-learning program for students who need to learn English. For instance, Cristina attends classes in which a specialized instructor in English learning applies instruction so Cristina can develop her language skills — speaking, reading, and writing.
  • Inclusion of support so that students who learn English are also successful in language arts. For example, Cristina's teacher includes lesson plans that help English learners develop the needed vocabulary or expressions for history, science, and other subjects that are part of language arts.
  • Instruction and support for oral and written academic language. To illustrate this, Cristina's teachers plan lessons that get Cristina to use academic language orally and written (e.g., oral presentations and compositions).

All of these points show the relation between ELD and ELA. However, you also have to know about the distinction between the achievements in each area.

Distinction Between ELA and ELD Achievement

While we know now that ELA and ELD standards support each other in California, there is a distinction between English learners' achievement of California ELD standards and achievement of ELA standards. This simply means that a grade or score that represents a student's achievement in language skills is different from the student's grade in history or science.

Depending on which grade students are in, there are specific ELA and ELD achievement standards. Each achievement standard is separate from the other. For example, Cristina is a student in third grade. For this grade, Cristina would ideally achieve the following standards, which are separate for ELD and ELA:

  • Regarding ELD: ability to interact in English in many ways (listening actively, reading, and identifying implicit and explicit information).
  • Regarding ELA: ability to express knowledge of history both orally and in writing, and ability to comprehend information about topics related to the subject of history.

Despite the fact that the achievement standards are independent for ELD and for ELA, you can see the connection between the two. In Cristina's example, the fact that she has learned to listen actively in the English language also impacts her ability to comprehend history topics in English.

Lesson Summary

English Language Development (ELD) standards are the set of skills a student should have to express ideas efficiently and confidently in English (e.g., ability to read, write, and speak). English Language Arts (ELA) standards are the set of literacy skills in subjects such as history/social studies, science and technical subjects. ELA and ELD are connected, and California teachers work to provide students with skills in both areas.

The California Department of Education provides teachers with a blueprint in a document called Reading/Language Arts Framework for California Public Schools so they know how to plan lessons to achieve goals in both areas. Its chapter 7 concentrates on Universal Access to the Language Arts Curriculum. This framework indicates that ELD supports ELA through instruction, but achievement standards in ELD are separate from ELA achievement standards.
