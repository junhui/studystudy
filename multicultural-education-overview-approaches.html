What is Multicultural Education?

The definition of multicultural education or multiculturalism education is a method that celebrates differences between cultures while challenging all forms of discrimination in education. When applied to education, the term multi-cultural can be defined as the inclusion of the vision that different human societies have about the world. Multicultural education is a method in which students are exposed to these different visions throughout their education. The broad purpose of this method is that students will learn how various cultures perceive certain aspects of life, such as mathematics, politics, religion, and art. There are several main objectives of the multicultural approach to education, but the overarching desire is to increase one's cosmopolitanism.

Goals of the Multicultural Approach

There are two primary goals of a multicultural education: celebrating all cultures and challenging all forms of discrimination, including in the workplace, educational spaces, public environments, and other places.

Stated and implied goals from multicultural education programs throughout the world include:

  • Empowerment of students and their guardians
  • Increase in cultural pluralism
  • Expanded knowledge about many cultures, groups, and societies
  • Professional practice that is increasingly guided by increased multicultural knowledge
  • Increased civic action

Applying Banks' Multicultural Education

James A. Banks, a university educator and expert on cultural instructional design, developed four main theories of multicultural education. Each of these progresses on the level preceding it, and each has its benefits and drawbacks. The four approaches to multicultural education are:

  • Contributions approach: This program of multicultural education focuses on individuals rather than groups. It provides examples of notable actions of individuals from different cultures. It also notes the contributions of individuals from other races than the majority to a specific field of information. For example, educators who use this approach discuss the contributions of Booker T. Washington to biology. However, these educators do not alter the goals or lesson plans of their curriculum. Banks refers to this approach as the one that requires the least amount of effort.
  • Additive approach: The additive approach, sometimes referred to as the ethnic additive approach, involves adding multicultural concepts to the curriculum of a course while maintaining the same overall course goals. For example, a teacher includes the account of African American slaves during the African American Revolution to enhance students' understanding of the events during the war. However, the educator does not assign any importance to these views and students are not required to look into them in any detail.
  • Transformation approach: Educators attempt to change the attitudes of their students regarding differences between cultures. The most common method used in this approach encourages students to use other cultures' perspectives of problems and concepts they learn about in class. An example of this approach would be if educators discussed the events of the colonization of America from the perspective of Native Americans. For example, the oft-whitewashed story of Thanksgiving would be discussed as one step in creating a trend of white settler colonist encroachment on the land and resources of indigenous native ethnic groups. Then, the educator devotes a module or unit to examining conflicts between cultures that exist in the same space. The underlying motivation for this approach to multicultural education is a desire to promote an appreciation for cultural diversity and pluralism.
  • Social action approach: This approach is a step beyond the transformative approach. After teaching about cultural conflict through an examination of interactions between Native Americans and pilgrim settlers, the instructor encourages their students to take action in various ways. For example, they could organize a multicultural celebration in their community or express their opinions on pluralism in an artistic work or a letter to a newspaper.

Examples of Multicultural Teaching in Practice

Multicultural teaching and education programs have been used in a variety of contexts. Examples of the above approaches include:

  • Contributions approach: Teaching students about the life and sacrifice of Crispus Attucks, who was the first American to die in the American Revolution. However, the status of Crispus Attucks as someone of African American and Wampanoag ethnicities is not discussed. Also, the implications of this event in the racial policies during the American Revolution are completely avoided.
  • Additive approach: Adding external readings to a course page about a historical event while not requiring knowledge of those readings in coursework or other educational evaluations.
  • Transformation approach: Conducting a critical analysis of the content of a course curriculum and altering the stated goals and course objectives to foster multiculturalism.
  • Social action approach: Incorporating student cultures in classroom activities, especially if these activities are related to extracurricular opportunities for cultural expression and multicultural cooperation.


Multicultural events such as this are the perfect example of the social action approach to multicultural education.


The Multicultural Approach Debate

While there are many benefits to getting a multicultural education, there are also significant criticisms of the multicultural approach. The benefits of multicultural education programs include that they level the playing field for all students and incorporate previously overlooked cultures.

The most major criticisms of this approach include the argument that it divides social groups rather than uniting them. Critics state that deliberate multicultural approaches to educating students about other cultures can create an atmosphere of comparison and introduce a subjective rather than objective view of culture. Another criticism is that there is a major difficulty in deciding if something is equal or fair.

Lesson Summary

Multicultural education celebrates differences and challenges discrimination in all its forms. It is a deliberate teaching method in which an educator exposes students in their class to perspectives on one topic from a variety of cultures. Through this process, students can learn about pluralism. They can also examine the differences and similarities between the economics, societal infrastructure, religion, and goals of different cultures and societies. James A. Banks, an African American educator and expert on cultural instructional design, created the multicultural theory of education. He created the four approaches of multicultural education to explore how individuals learned about different cultures and internalized the things they learned. There are significant criticisms of multicultural education, including that it creates an atmosphere of comparison and reduces objectivity.

The different multicultural approaches to education proposed by James A. Banks include the contributions approach, which refers to the contributions of individuals from minority races or cultures to a certain field of knowledge. This approach also emphasizes people who fit the hero archetype from minority cultures. However, the curriculum of the lesson or course is not significantly changed. The additive approach consists of adding external readings to the review of a historical fact regardless of whether one does not have in-depth knowledge about said readings. In the transformation approach, educators seek to change the perspectives of their students regarding other cultures and how they view cultural differences. They do this by deliberately creating a curriculum that encourages students to think about concepts and solve problems through the perspective of another culture. Finally, the social action approach includes both educators and students engaging in action. Students take what they learn from the course and engage in their society in various ways.

What is Multicultural Education?

Multicultural education is an educational philosophy that focuses on celebrating cultural differences while also recognizing the importance of challenging all forms of discrimination based on race, gender, age, religion, ability or sexual orientation.

Approaches to Multicultural Education

Dr. James A. Banks, educator and author of over 20 books on multicultural education, has identified four approaches that teachers can use for integrating multiculturalism into their curriculum. Let's look at each approach, one at a time:

Contributions

Teachers using this approach provide examples of the relevant contributions and heroic accomplishments of people from different races and cultures, without changing the lesson plan or goals of the unit being taught. For example, in a science unit students could learn of the contributions of scientists from different countries or American ethnicities.

Additive

This approach requires adding cultural realities that traditionally are left out of a curriculum that focuses on a traditional holiday, like Independence Day or Thanksgiving. Providing stories about how Native Americans were involved in the first Thanksgiving celebration would be one way to add a multicultural element. The Additive approach does not change the overall curriculum.

Transformation

The transformation approach seeks to change the attitudes about cultural differences by using a different curriculum, one that encourages students to view problems and concepts from the perspective of different cultures. For example, if students were studying the American Indian wars, they would consider the views of the settlers, soldiers, and also the native peoples.

Social Action

The social action approach uses the concepts from the transformation curriculum and takes it a step further, where students take action for social change. After acquiring the necessary knowledge about something in their community that needs change, students get involved in activities that may effect that change, such as writing letters to senators or taking the time to reach out and befriend students of different races or ethnic backgrounds.

Ideas for Multicultural Integration

Now that we know what multicultural education is, let's look at a few ideas for integrating it into the classroom.

Cultural

Ask students to view animated films about historical figures, such as Pocahontas or other films that might distort or stereotype people. Have students research the history of the main characters before they view the films, then ask them to compare their research with what was in the film.

Comic Books

Have students read and review a variety of comic books and critique how women and people of minority groups are portrayed along with how violence and heroism show bias with gender, race, religion, age, and other factors, such as disabilities.

Essays to Comic Strips

Ask students to write essays based on their own cultural experiences, whether it's something they've witnessed or experienced themselves. If they're comfortable with the idea, suggest that they develop a comic strip of the experience. You can expand the unit with a drawing skills lesson and have an exhibit when it's complete.

Lesson Summary

Multicultural education celebrates cultural differences and recognizes the importance of challenging all forms of discrimination based on race, gender, age, religion, ability or sexual orientation. Dr. James A. Banks identified four different approaches to making education multicultural.

You can take the contributions approach, where you provide examples of contributions from other cultures while keeping the curriculum the same. A similar approach is the additive approach, where you add cultural realities that are normally left out of the curriculum. Another way to make education multicultural is to go the transformation route, meaning you change students' attitudes by viewing problems from different perspectives. A different option is social action, where you have students take action for social change by having the students do things like write letters or get to know people from different cultures.

To integrate these ideas in your lessons, you can have students share their own experiences and share them with the class through essays or comic strips. You can also have them view media, like movies or comic books, and then have them analyze them for inaccuracies and biases.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the benefits of multicultural education?

The main benefits of multicultural education are that discrimination in all forms decreases in communities in which it is practiced, and that students develop more pluralist paradigms. They also become more involved in their society.

What is the main goal of multicultural education?

The main goals of multicultural education are to increase cultural appreciation and prevent discrimination in all its forms. Multicultural education aims to increase pluralism in a society.

What are the 4 approaches to multicultural education?

There are four main approaches to multicultural education. They are the contributions, additive, transformative, and social action approaches. The contributive approach requires the least amount of effort, and the social action requires effort on part of the students and the educator.
